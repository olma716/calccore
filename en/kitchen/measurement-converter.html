Why you need a cooking measurement converter

Many recipes, especially from English-language sources, use volume measurements — cups, tablespoons, teaspoons — while other countries typically weigh ingredients in grams. This converter helps accurately translate one system into the other.

Why ingredient density matters

Volume and weight are different physical quantities, related through density. A cup of flour weighs significantly less than a cup of honey or sugar, so there's no universal "1 cup = X grams" conversion — it all depends on the specific ingredient. This calculator accounts for the average density of popular ingredients for a more accurate conversion.

Standard volumes used by the calculator

  • 1 cup (US) = 240 ml
  • 1 tablespoon (tbsp) = 15 ml
  • 1 teaspoon (tsp) = 5 ml
  • 1 fluid ounce (fl oz) = ~29.57 ml

Density values are population averages and may vary slightly depending on ingredient moisture, how densely it's packed into the measuring cup, and the specific brand. For critical recipes (such as precise baking), a kitchen scale is recommended.

FAQ

Why does conversion depend on the selected ingredient?

Different ingredients have different densities — a cup of flour weighs less than a cup of sugar or honey, so converting volume to weight varies by ingredient.

Which cup size is used — US or metric?

The calculator uses the standard US cup (240 ml), which is most common in English-language recipes.

How accurate is this conversion?

The values are based on average ingredient densities and may vary slightly depending on how densely the product is packed into the measuring cup.
