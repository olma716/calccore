What the stoichiometry calculator does

The “Amount of substance” tab links the mass, amount of substance, number of particles and gas volume. “From an equation” finds the mass, amount and volume of all other substances from the known amount of one substance in a reaction. “Limiting reagent” compares the reactants, finds the product that forms and the reaction yield. The molar mass can be entered as a formula.

Basic formulas

Amount of substance n = m / M, number of particles N = n · Nₐ (Nₐ = 6.022·10²³ mol⁻¹), gas volume at standard conditions V = n · Vₘ (Vₘ = 22.414 L/mol; 22.4 at school). For 100 g of CaCO₃ (M = 100.086 g/mol): n = 100 / 100.086 ≈ 0.9991 mol.

Calculating from a reaction equation

The coefficients in the equation show the molar ratios. For CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ all coefficients equal 1, so 0.9991 mol of carbonate gives the same amount of CO₂: m = 0.9991 · 44.009 ≈ 43.97 g and the volume is V = 0.9991 · 22.414 ≈ 22.39 L. If the substances do not react 1 : 1, the calculator multiplies the amount by the ratio of the coefficients.

Limiting reagent

The reactant that runs out first limits the amount of product. For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ compare n / ν: for 28 g of nitrogen it is 0.9995, for 10 g of hydrogen 4.96 / 3 ≈ 1.65. Nitrogen has the smaller value, so it is limiting, and about 1.96 mol of hydrogen is left over. The theoretical yield of NH₃ is 2 · 0.9995 ≈ 1.999 mol, or 34.04 g.

Reaction yield

The product actually obtained is usually less than the theoretical amount. The yield is η = m(actual) / m(theoretical) · 100 %: if 25 g were obtained out of the theoretically possible 34.05 g of ammonia, η ≈ 73.4 %. Conversely, for a known η the calculator finds the mass you will get in practice.

A typical problem step by step

How many grams of CaO form when 100 g of CaCO₃ decompose? The equation CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ has all coefficients equal to 1. The amount of carbonate is n = 100 / 100.086 ≈ 0.9991 mol, so CaO is also 0.9991 mol and its mass is m = 0.9991 · 56.077 ≈ 56.03 g. The calculator does these steps itself and also shows the mass and volume of CO₂.

Gas volume and conditions

The volume of a gas depends on temperature and pressure. The calculator works at standard conditions (0 °C, 101.325 kPa) where 1 mol takes 22.414 L. At room temperature (25 °C, the same pressure) the molar volume is about 24.5 L/mol, so for such conditions the volume has to be recalculated separately.

Gas volume is calculated at standard conditions (0 °C, 101.325 kPa): Vₘ = 22.414 L/mol, and 22.4 L/mol in the school set. Choose the set in the settings.

FAQ

What is stoichiometry?

The part of chemistry about the quantitative relations between substances in reactions, set by the coefficients of the equation.

How do I find the amount of substance?

n = m / M. For 100 g of CaCO₃ it is about 0.9991 mol.

What volume does 1 mole of gas occupy?

At standard conditions (0 °C, 101.325 kPa) 22.414 L; school problems use 22.4 L.

How do I calculate the mass of a product from an equation?

Find n of the known substance, multiply by the ratio of the coefficients, then by the molar mass of the product.

Do I have to set the coefficients myself?

No, the calculator sets them automatically. It is enough to write the equation with formulas.

How do I find the limiting reagent?

For each reactant divide the amount of substance by its coefficient. The one with the smallest ratio runs out first.

What is the reaction yield?

The percentage of the product actually obtained out of the theoretically possible: η = m(actual) / m(theoretical) · 100 %.

What does an empty reactant field mean?

A reactant without an amount is taken as in excess and does not limit the reaction — for example, oxygen from the air.

Can I enter the molar mass as a formula?

Yes, in the “M” field enter a formula such as CaCO3 or H2SO4.

How do school and standard values differ?

In the school set atomic masses are rounded, Vₘ = 22.4 L/mol and Nₐ = 6.02·10²³; the standard set uses IUPAC values.

Why does the gas volume depend on the conditions?

Because the volume of a gas changes with temperature and pressure. At standard conditions 1 mol of any ideal gas takes 22.414 L, at 25 °C about 24.5 L.

What is an excess reagent?

A reactant of which there is more than needed to react completely with the limiting one: it remains after the reaction. The calculator shows how much is left.
