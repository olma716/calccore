What the calculator does

The calculator suggests what to use instead of an ingredient you do not have and works out how much to take for your amount. Choose an ingredient (egg, butter, buttermilk, sour cream, sugar, baking powder and others), enter the amount and the unit, and you see every substitute with the amounts, a note on the effect on taste and texture and a link to the source. The database holds 23 ingredients and 61 substitutes in seven categories.

Where the substitutes come from

Every substitute comes from a named source and is linked. The base of the database is the lists from Utah State University Extension and Colorado State University Extension: substitutes that agree in both carry two sources, and where the amounts differ both options are shown. They are supplemented by NC State Extension, University of Wisconsin–Madison Extension (egg substitutes), King Arthur Baking (buttermilk), Baking Sense and America's Test Kitchen. Substitutes without a source (for example stevia instead of sugar, which depends on the product) are not included.

Egg substitutes

An egg plays different roles, so the substitute depends on the recipe. For binding, ground flax or chia seeds work (1 tbsp of seeds and 3 tbsp of water per egg, left to sit for 5–10 minutes): for two eggs that is 2 tbsp of flax and 6 tbsp of water. For moisture take ¼ cup of mashed banana, applesauce or yogurt per egg. For meringues and brownies aquafaba works (3 tbsp whipped per egg), for leavening baking powder with water and oil, and for dense bakes nut butter. The sources give two versions of the baking powder mix, so both are shown.

Butter, fats and dairy

Butter can be replaced by margarine 1:1, or by oil: one source advises ¾ cup per cup of butter and another ⅞, so both are shown. For vegan baking vegetable shortening works 1:1. Buttermilk is replaced by milk with vinegar or lemon juice (1 tbsp per cup, milk to one cup, rest 5–15 minutes) or by yogurt, and in King Arthur's test a mix of Greek yogurt and milk 1:2 did best. Heavy cream can be made from ¾ cup of milk and ⅓ cup of melted butter, but this cream will not whip.

Sugar, honey and flour

A cup of sugar is replaced by ¾ cup of honey (reduce the other liquid by ¼ cup), and a cup of brown sugar is a cup of sugar with ¼ cup of molasses. Cake flour is replaced by sifted all-purpose flour minus 2 tbsp per cup. For thickening, 1 tbsp of cornstarch is replaced by 2 tbsp of all-purpose flour or 1 tbsp of tapioca starch.

Baking powder, chocolate and spices

Baking powder (1 tsp) can be made from ¼ tsp of baking soda and cream of tartar. The sources disagree here: ½ tsp (Baking Sense) or ⅝ tsp (USU and NC State Extension), so both are shown. There are also substitutes with soured milk, vinegar or molasses. Unsweetened chocolate (1 oz) is replaced by 3 tbsp of cocoa with 1 tbsp of fat, and pumpkin pie spice is made from cinnamon, ginger, allspice and nutmeg.

Scaling to your amount

The sources give substitutes per one “base”: 1 egg, 1 cup, 1 tbsp, 1 tsp or 1 oz. The calculator multiplies every part by your factor and shows the result in handy units with practical fractions (¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, ¾). In the sources a cup is 240 ml, so on the “Substitute” tab the amounts are rescaled to your cup (250, 200, 240 ml or the British 284 ml). You can write the amount as a fraction: 1/2, 1 1/2.

Limits

Substitutions change taste and texture, and changing several ingredients at once makes the result less predictable: change one ingredient at a time and try a small batch. The ratios are for ordinary baking and cooking. The labels “vegan”, “egg-free” and “dairy-free” describe the substitute itself, not the finished dish. With allergies and diets check the ingredient labels, margarine and ready-made mixes included.

FAQ

What can I use instead of an egg in baking?

It depends on the egg's role: ground flax or chia (1 tbsp and 3 tbsp of water) for binding, ¼ cup of banana, applesauce or yogurt for moisture, aquafaba for meringues, baking powder with water and oil for lightness.

What can I use instead of buttermilk?

Milk with vinegar or lemon juice: 1 tbsp of acid and milk to one cup, rest 5–15 minutes. Yogurt or a mix of Greek yogurt and milk 1:2 also work.

What can I use instead of baking powder?

For 1 tsp of baking powder take ¼ tsp of baking soda and ½ tsp of cream of tartar (some sources say ⅝ tsp), or soda with soured milk, vinegar or molasses.

What can I use instead of butter?

Margarine 1:1, vegetable shortening 1:1 or oil: ¾ cup per cup of butter (another source says ⅞). Applesauce can replace up to half the butter in quick breads.

How do I replace sugar with honey?

For a cup of sugar take ¾ cup of honey and reduce the other liquid in the recipe by ¼ cup. In baking add ½ tsp of soda per cup of honey.

What can I use instead of brown sugar?

A cup of granulated sugar with ¼ cup of molasses. Use 1.5–2 tbsp of molasses per cup, less for light brown sugar and more for dark.

What can I use instead of sour cream?

Plain yogurt 1:1; Greek yogurt is closest in thickness. A sour milk and butter option exists too.

What can I use instead of cornstarch?

For 1 tbsp of cornstarch take 2 tbsp of all-purpose flour or 1 tbsp of tapioca starch (gluten-free).

Can I replace several ingredients at once?

You can, but the result will be less predictable. It is better to change one ingredient at a time and try a small batch.

Are the substitutes suitable for vegan baking?

Yes, the “By diet” tab lists vegan substitutes. The label describes the substitute itself, and the ingredients of ready-made products (margarine, for example) should be checked separately.

Why are several options shown for some substitutes?

The sources disagree: for example for oil instead of butter ¾ or ⅞ cup, for baking powder ½ or ⅝ tsp of cream of tartar. Both are shown with a link to the source.

How many ml are in a cup in the substitutions?

In the US sources a cup is 240 ml. On the “Substitute” tab the amounts are rescaled to your cup: 250, 200 ml, the US 240 ml or the British 284 ml.
