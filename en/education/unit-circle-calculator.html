What the unit circle calculator does

The calculator finds the sine, cosine, tangent and cotangent of any angle, shows it on the unit circle with projections and the tangent line, converts degrees to radians, and gives the quadrant and the reference angle. For multiples of 15° the values are exact, for example sin 150° = 1/2, cos 150° = −√3/2, sin 15° = (√6 − √2)/4. There is also the graph of y = A·sin(Bx + C) + D and solving the equations sin x = a, cos x = a, tan x = a.

The unit circle

The unit circle is the circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. The point on it for the angle θ has coordinates (cos θ; sin θ). The tangent is the ratio sin θ / cos θ and is shown as a segment on the tangent line x = 1. Signs: sin is positive in quadrants I and II, cos in I and IV, tan in I and III.

Degrees and radians

π radians = 180°. To convert degrees to radians multiply by π/180: 150° = 5π/6. To convert radians to degrees multiply by 180/π: π/3 = 60°. Angles that differ by 360° (2π) have the same function values and are called coterminal.

The graph of y = A·sin(Bx + C) + D

|A| is the amplitude, T = 2π/|B| the period (π/|B| for tangent), x₀ = −C/B the phase shift, D the vertical shift and the midline. For y = 2 sin(2x − π/3) + 1 the amplitude is 2, the period π, the shift π/6 to the right, and the range [−1; 3]. A negative A reflects the graph about the midline.

Solving sin x = a

The equation sin x = a has solutions when |a| ≤ 1: x = (−1)ⁿ·arcsin a + πn. The equation cos x = a: x = ±arccos a + 2πn. The equation tan x = a: x = arctan a + πn. For sin x = 1/2 the solutions on [0°; 360°) are 30° and 150°, that is π/6 and 5π/6. For values that are not table values the angles are approximate.

All calculations run in your browser. The values for multiples of 15° come exactly from a table, the rest are rounded to the chosen number of places.

FAQ

What is the unit circle?

It is the circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. The point on it for the angle θ has coordinates (cos θ; sin θ).

What is sin 150°?

sin 150° = sin 30° = 1/2: in quadrant II the sine is positive, the reference angle is 30°.

What is cos 150°?

cos 150° = −cos 30° = −√3/2: in quadrant II the cosine is negative.

How do I convert degrees to radians?

Multiply by π/180. For example 150° = 150·π/180 = 5π/6.

How do I convert radians to degrees?

Multiply by 180/π. For example π/3 = 60°.

How do I find the reference angle?

It is the acute angle between the ray and the x-axis: 180° − θ in quadrant II, θ − 180° in III, 360° − θ in IV.

How do I find the period of sin(Bx)?

T = 2π/|B|. For sin 2x the period is π. For tan(Bx) the period is π/|B|.

How do I solve sin x = 1/2?

x = π/6 + 2πn and x = 5π/6 + 2πn, where n is a whole number. On [0°; 360°) these are 30° and 150°.

Why has sin x = 2 no solutions?

Sine and cosine always lie between −1 and 1, so the value 2 is impossible.

Which angles have exact values?

Multiples of 15°: 0°, 15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, 90° and their reflections in the other quadrants.

Why does tan 90° not exist?

Because cos 90° = 0 and the tangent is sin/cos: a division by zero. Likewise cot 0° and cot 180° do not exist.

Can I enter angles with π?

Yes: π/6, 5π/6, -pi/4, 3.5π. Marks such as 150° and 150 rad also work.
