What the force calculator does

The calculator solves dynamics problems: Newton's second law and weight, friction, the elastic force by Hooke's law, universal gravitation, density and pressure, hydrostatic pressure and buoyancy. Enter the known quantities and the calculator finds the unknown one, showing the formula and the substitution.

Newton's second law and weight

The force acting on a body equals the mass times the acceleration: F = m · a. For m = 5 kg and a = 3 m/s² the force is 15 N. The weight of a body at rest on a horizontal support is P = m · g: for 5 kg and g = 9.8 m/s² it is 49 N. 1 kgf = 9.80665 N.

Friction

The sliding friction force is F = μ · N, where N is the normal force of the support and μ the friction coefficient. On a horizontal surface N = m · g. For μ = 0.2 and m = 10 kg with g = 9.8 m/s² the friction is 0.2 · 98 = 19.6 N.

Hooke's law and spring energy

The elastic force is proportional to the extension: F = k · x. For k = 400 N/m and x = 5 cm = 0.05 m the force is 20 N, and the spring energy E = k · x² / 2 = 0.5 J. Hooke's law holds for small deformations.

Universal gravitation

The gravitational force between two bodies is F = G · m₁ · m₂ / r², where G = 6.6743·10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². For the Earth (5.972·10²⁴ kg) and the Moon (7.348·10²² kg) at 3.844·10⁸ m the force is about 1.98·10²⁰ N. Enter large numbers as 5.972e24.

Density, pressure and buoyancy

Density ρ = m / V: aluminium of mass 540 kg in 0.2 m³ has ρ = 2700 kg/m³. Pressure p = F / S. The pressure of a fluid at depth h is p = ρ · g · h; for water (1000 kg/m³) at 10 m with g = 9.8 it is 98 000 Pa = 98 kPa. The buoyant force Fₐ = ρ · g · V is the weight of the displaced fluid.

Weight, mass and the normal force

The mass of a body is measured in kilograms and does not depend on the place, while the weight P = m · g is a force in newtons with which the body presses on its support. With g = 9.8 m/s² a 10 kg body weighs about 98 N. The sliding friction force F = μN depends on the normal force N, which equals mg only on a horizontal surface; on an inclined plane it is smaller because part of the weight is carried along the slope. That is why in the “Friction” mode for a horizontal surface it is enough to give the mass and the coefficient μ.

For weight and fluid pressure the value of g (9.8, 9.81 or 10 m/s²) is chosen in the settings. All numbers are exact.

FAQ

What is Newton's second law?

The acceleration of a body is directly proportional to the force and inversely proportional to the mass: a = F / m, that is F = m · a.

What is the difference between weight and mass?

Mass is measured in kilograms and does not depend on the place; weight P = m · g is the force with which a body presses on a support, measured in newtons.

How do I find the friction force?

F = μ · N. On a horizontal surface N = m · g. For μ = 0.2 and m = 10 kg it is 19.6 N (g = 9.8).

What is Hooke's law?

The elastic force is directly proportional to the extension: F = k · x.

How do I find the spring energy?

E = k · x² / 2. For k = 400 N/m and x = 5 cm it is 0.5 J.

What is the gravitational constant?

G = 6.6743·10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². The calculator uses exactly this value.

How do I enter very large numbers?

With the notation 5.972e24 (that is 5.972·10²⁴). The answer is also shown as a·10ⁿ.

How do I find density?

ρ = m / V. For 540 kg and 0.2 m³ it is 2700 kg/m³. For water it is about 1000 kg/m³.

How do I find pressure?

p = F / S. For a fluid p = ρ · g · h. The unit is the pascal: 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

What is the buoyant force?

The upward force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid: Fₐ = ρ · g · V.

How many newtons are in a kilogram-force?

1 kgf = 9.80665 N.

Which value of g should I use?

9.8 m/s², more precisely 9.81, and 10 in school problems. The value is chosen in the settings.

Why is weight not equal to mass?

Mass is a measure of the amount of matter in kilograms, while weight is a force in newtons: P = m · g. On the Moon the mass is the same, but the weight is about 6 times smaller.

What is the difference between static and sliding friction?

Static friction keeps a body from starting to move and can vary up to a maximum, while sliding friction acts on a body that is already moving and is usually slightly smaller than the maximum static friction.
