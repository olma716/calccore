What the wallpaper calculator counts

The calculator works out how many rolls of wallpaper are needed for one or several rooms (up to six), as well as paste and the cost. For every room or a single wall you enter the dimensions and the width of the openings, and also the roll size, the type of the pattern match, the trimming allowance and the reserve. The result is the number of rolls, strips, the waste, the area of the wallpaper bought and, if you give the coverage of a pack, the amount of paste. There are no built-in prices: the cost is counted from your prices in hryvnia, dollars or euros.

How strips and rolls are counted

Wallpaper is hung in vertical strips, so the strips are counted first: the perimeter of the room without the width of doors and windows is divided by the roll width and rounded up. The length of a strip is the ceiling height plus the trimming allowance. A roll gives a whole number of strips, and the end that is too short for one more strip goes to waste.

Example: a room of 4×3.5 m, a ceiling of 2.4 m, a door of 0.9 m, a roll of 0.53×10.05 m, a 5 cm allowance. The perimeter without the door is 14.1 m, which is 27 strips of 2.45 m. With no pattern match a roll gives 4 strips, so 7 rolls are needed.

Pattern match: straight and drop

If the wallpaper has a pronounced pattern, the neighbouring strips have to be matched. The pattern repeats with a step (the repeat), and every strip has to start at the same point of the pattern. So before every strip a piece of the roll is cut off to the needed phase of the pattern. With a straight match all the strips start at one phase; with a drop match every second strip is shifted by half the repeat. The calculator cuts the rolls one after another and counts this waste instead of just rounding the height.

For the same room with a 2.4 m ceiling and a 32 cm repeat: with no match 4 strips per roll and 7 rolls, with a straight match 3 strips per roll and 9 rolls, with a drop match 3 and 9 rolls.

Worked example

Two rooms: 4×3.5 m and 3.2×2.8 m, a ceiling of 2.7 m, a door of 0.9 m in each. A roll of 0.53×10.05 m, a straight match of 32 cm, a 5 cm allowance, a 5% reserve. The strips are 27 and 21, 48 in total, the length of a strip is 2.75 m. A roll gives 3 strips, so 16 rolls are needed, 17 with the reserve. The waste is 19%. The area of the walls is 68.0 m², and at a pack coverage of 25 m² the paste is 3 packs. At 1,200 ₴ per roll and 180 ₴ per pack of paste the cost is 20,940 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. Always check the roll size and the pattern repeat on the label.

FAQ

How do I calculate the number of rolls of wallpaper for a room?

The perimeter of the room minus the width of doors and windows is divided by the roll width (rounded up): that is the number of strips. Then see how many strips of the length of the ceiling height plus the allowance fit into a roll, and divide the strips by that number.

How many rolls of wallpaper for a room of 4×3.5 m?

For a 2.4 m ceiling, a 0.9 m door and a roll of 0.53×10.05 m that is 27 strips: 7 rolls with no pattern match, 9 with a straight match of a 32 cm repeat and 9 with a drop match. For another height and roll the result is different, so enter your own data.

What is the pattern repeat and where do I find it?

It is the distance at which the pattern repeats along the roll. It is printed on the label of the wallpaper in centimetres. The larger the repeat, the more waste for the match.

What is the difference between a straight and a drop match?

With a straight match the pattern of the neighbouring strips is at the same height; with a drop match every second strip is shifted by half the repeat (half-drop). A drop match usually gives a little more waste; the type is on the label.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

The calculator subtracts the sum of the widths of the openings from the perimeter, so no strips are counted there. The strips above doors and under windows are not counted separately: they are usually cut from the offcuts, which gives a small margin.

What trimming allowance should I use?

Usually a few centimetres at the top and the bottom for trimming at the ceiling and the skirting. The field is empty by default; enter the value your decorator uses, the total for the top and the bottom.

How many extra rolls should I buy?

The reserve is set as a percentage of the number of rolls and rounded up. It covers cutting mistakes and future repairs: rolls of another batch can differ in shade.

What sizes of wallpaper rolls are there?

The most common are 0.53×10.05 m, 0.70×10.05 m, 1.06×10.05 m and 1.06×25 m. Your size is on the label; you can enter it as your own.

Why does a roll give fewer strips than its length divided by the height?

Because with a patterned wallpaper a piece is cut off before every strip to the needed phase of the pattern, and at the end of the roll there is a piece shorter than a strip. The calculator cuts the roll one strip after another and counts these losses.

How much paste is needed for wallpaper?

The area of the walls is divided by the coverage of one pack, printed on the pack of paste, and rounded up. The calculator takes the area of the walls as the perimeter without the openings times the ceiling height. The coverage depends on the maker and the type of the wallpaper, so there is no default value.

Does the calculator account for niches, projections and ceilings?

No. The calculator counts vertical strips along the walls. For a niche or a projection you can add a separate wall; wallpaper for a ceiling is not counted here.

Why should the rolls be from one batch?

Rolls from different batches can differ slightly in shade, and it shows on the wall. The batch number is on the label, so take all the rolls from one.
