      How the range is calculated

      The basic formula is simple: range = usable energy ÷ consumption × 100, where usable energy is battery capacity × battery health × (charge level − reserve). For example, for a 60 kWh battery and 17 kWh/100 km consumption the range on a full charge is about 353 km. The result is then multiplied by temperature and speed factors. You can fill in the battery capacity by picking a car model from the list (with search).

      What affects real-world range

      Temperature. According to Geotab (an analysis of over 5 million trips), between 10 and 31 °C an EV reaches on average 100% of its rated range, and about 54% at −15 °C. In AAA dynamometer tests (2019) range dropped 41% at −7 °C with the heater on and 12% without it; at +35 °C with air conditioning by 17%, without it by 4%. The calculator draws a piecewise line through these points: for 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km at −7 °C with heating you get about 208 km, without heating — 311 km. If your car has a heat pump, tick the heat pump option: it uses less energy in the cold, so the same −7 °C gives about 274 km (a Recurrent-based estimate: roughly −17% at 0 °C and −22% at −7 °C).

      Speed. Most of the energy on a highway goes to overcoming air resistance, which grows roughly with the square of speed. The calculator uses a simplified model of a typical EV: highway at 110 km/h — factor 0.63 (about 222 km in the same example), 130 km/h — 0.50 (about 176 km). In the city the factor is capped at 1.10 because stop-and-go and acceleration reduce the benefit of low speed. There are ready modes from 80 to 140 km/h, and you can also enter your own speed from 20 to 200 km/h.

      Reserve and battery health. If you don’t want to drain the battery below 10%, the range in the example drops to 318 km. With battery wear down to 85% of the original capacity — to 300 km.

      FAQ

      How is EV range calculated?

      Range = usable energy ÷ consumption × 100, where usable energy is battery capacity × battery health × (charge level − reserve). For 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km that is about 353 km on a full charge. The result is then multiplied by temperature and speed factors.

      Why does real range differ from the rated one?

      Rated figures (WLTP, EPA) are measured under controlled conditions. In real driving range is affected by temperature, speed, driving style, climate control, terrain, tires and load.

      How much does range drop in winter?

      According to Geotab, at −15 °C an EV reaches on average about 54% of its rated range. In AAA tests, at −7 °C the loss is about 41% with the heater and about 12% without it. For 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km the calculator gives about 208 km at −7 °C with heating versus 353 km in mild conditions, and about 274 km for a car with a heat pump.

      Does hot weather affect range?

      Yes, but less than cold. In AAA tests, at +35 °C with air conditioning range drops by about 17%, without it by 4%. For 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km at +35 °C that is about 293 km.

      How does speed affect range?

      Air resistance grows roughly with the square of speed, so highway consumption is much higher than in mixed driving. In the calculator’s simplified model: 80 km/h — factor 0.89, 90 — 0.80, 100 — 0.71, 110 — 0.63, 120 — 0.56, 130 — 0.50, 140 — 0.45. You can also enter your own speed from 20 to 200 km/h. For 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km that is about 222 km at 110 km/h and 176 km at 130 km/h.

      What is the charge reserve?

      It is the part of the charge you don’t want to use, for example 10% as a buffer before charging. The calculator shows the range down to this reserve: in the example with 60 kWh and 17 kWh/100 km a 10% reserve reduces the range from 353 to 318 km.

      What is battery health and how to account for it?

      It is the remaining capacity compared with a new battery (degradation). If the battery keeps 85% of its original capacity, the range in the 60 kWh example is about 300 km. You can take the value from diagnostics or the trip computer.

      How do I check whether the charge is enough for a trip?

      Enter the trip distance, temperature and driving mode. The calculator shows the energy needed, the charge on arrival and, if the charge is not enough, how many kWh to add, with a link to the charging cost calculation.

      Where can I find the car consumption (kWh/100 km)?

      From the spec sheet (WLTP) or the trip computer: the average consumption over recent trips. Real consumption is usually higher than the rated one, especially in winter and on the highway.

      Should I charge to 100% every day?

      For daily use 80–90% is usually recommended, keeping a full charge for long trips: this extends battery life.

      Where do the battery capacities for the car models come from?

      These are usable (net) capacities from public sources. For some models (Tesla, Chinese cars, different years and trims) the sources disagree, so the value is approximate: check it for your car against its documents and correct it manually if needed.
