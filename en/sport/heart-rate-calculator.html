What heart rate zones are

Heart rate zones are ranges of heart rate that match different training intensities. Training by heart rate means controlling effort not by speed or weight but by the heart's response: the same running speed can be easy on a rested day and hard when tired or hot, and the heart rate shows it. The classic split is five zones from recovery to maximum effort, each training a different quality.

Max heart rate: four formulas

Zones are counted from the maximum heart rate. The best-known Fox formula is 220 − age, but it is crude. The Tanaka formula (2001) — 208 − 0.7 × age — was derived from a meta-analysis of many studies and is usually more accurate for adults of different ages: it gives lower values than Fox under 40 and higher after 40. The HUNT formula is 211 − 0.64 × age, derived on a large sample of Norwegians. Gulati — 206 − 0.88 × age — is for women only. For a 30-year-old these are 187 (Tanaka), 190 (Fox), 192 (HUNT) and 180 (Gulati, woman) bpm. All are group averages: an individual maximum can differ by 10–20 bpm, so the best option is a value measured in a real all-out effort.

The Karvonen method and heart rate reserve

The simple method takes a percentage of the maximum. The Karvonen method also uses the resting heart rate: limit = resting rate + (maximum − resting rate) × share. The difference between the maximum and the resting rate is called the heart rate reserve. For a maximum of 187 and a resting rate of 60 the reserve is 127, and the five zones become 124–136, 137–149, 150–162, 163–174 and 175–187 bpm against 94–112, 113–131, 132–150, 151–168 and 169–187 by the simple method. Karvonen reflects individual fitness better: trained people have a lower resting rate, and the zones shift up.

Five intensity zones

Zone 1 (50–60%) — recovery, talking is effortless, effort 2–3 out of 10. Zone 2 (60–70%) — easy, full sentences are possible; the base of aerobic endurance. Zone 3 (70–80%) — moderate, short phrases. Zone 4 (80–90%) — hard, only a few words, develops speed and threshold. Zone 5 (90–100%) — maximum, talking is almost impossible; it is held in short intervals. The zones in the calculator do not overlap: the upper limit of a zone is rounded and the next zone starts 1 bpm higher. The descriptions of effort and the talk test are approximate.

The fat-burning zone is a simplification

The “fat-burning zone” usually means zone 2: in easy exercise the share of fat in the energy is higher. But for weight loss the total calorie deficit decides, and in harder zones more calories are burned in the same time. So easy zones are for the base and recovery, not “the only place where fat burns”. If your goal is weight loss, use the calorie calculator.

Threshold zones by LTHR

Trained runners often count zones not from the maximum but from the lactate threshold heart rate (LTHR). Per Friel it is estimated as the average heart rate of the last 20 minutes of a 30-minute steady run at maximum sustainable effort. Running zones as percentages of LTHR: below 85% — zone 1, 85–89 — 2, 90–94 — 3, 95–99 — 4, 100–102 — 5a, 103–106 — 5b, above 106 — 5c. For an LTHR of 165 that is zone 2: 140–148, zone 4: 157–164, threshold (5a): 165–169 bpm. This scale is for running; for cycling and swimming the percentages differ.

Accuracy and safety

The formulas give a group average, while heart rate depends on fatigue, stress, heat, caffeine, medication and fitness. So also rely on how you feel: the talk test and the effort scale. The result is not medical advice. With heart disease, hypertension or medication that affects heart rate (for example beta-blockers) agree the intensity with a doctor; a constant resting heart rate above 100 bpm is also worth discussing with one.

All values are estimates from statistical formulas. Your own max heart rate can differ by 10–20 bpm. This is not medical advice: with heart conditions or medication agree the intensity with a doctor.

FAQ

How do I calculate max heart rate?

The simplest is 220 − age (Fox). The Tanaka formula, 208 − 0.7 × age, is more accurate for most adults. The best option is a value measured in a real all-out effort.

Which max heart rate formula is the most accurate?

There is no single one: all give a group average, and an individual maximum differs by 10–20 bpm. Tanaka is usually more accurate than Fox, especially after 40.

What is the Karvonen formula?

Zone limit = resting rate + (maximum − resting rate) × percentage. It accounts for the resting heart rate, so it reflects individual fitness better.

What is heart rate reserve?

The difference between the max heart rate and the resting heart rate. Karvonen zones are percentages of this reserve.

How do I measure my resting heart rate?

In the morning right after waking, lying still, count your pulse for a minute for several days in a row and take the average.

How many heart rate zones are there and what are they?

Five: recovery (50–60%), easy (60–70%), moderate (70–80%), hard (80–90%) and maximum (90–100%).

In which zone do you burn fat?

Fat is used in all zones; its share is higher in the easy one (zone 2). For weight loss the total calorie deficit decides, not the “fat-burning zone”.

Which zone should I choose for endurance?

Most endurance training volume is done in zones 1–2, sometimes with short bursts in zones 4–5.

What is the talk test?

In zone 2 you can speak in full sentences, in zone 3 in short phrases, in zone 4 a few words, and in zone 5 you can hardly speak.

What is LTHR and how do I find it?

Lactate threshold heart rate. As a rough guide, it is the average heart rate of the last 20 minutes of a 30-minute steady run at maximum sustainable effort.

Why is my heart rate in training different?

Heart rate depends on fatigue, stress, heat, caffeine, medication and sleep. The formulas are only a starting point, so also go by how you feel.

Is it safe to train in zones 4 and 5?

For a healthy person in moderate amounts yes, but you need a warm-up and gradual progress. With heart disease or hypertension agree the intensity with a doctor.
