What the converter does

The converter converts cooking measures by ingredient: cups and spoons to grams, grams to cups, milliliters to ounces and back. Volume and weight are linked through density, and every ingredient has its own: a cup of flour and a cup of sugar weigh differently. So to convert between volume and weight you need to choose an ingredient, while for volume to volume or weight to weight it does not matter. The database has 85 ingredients: flour, grains, sugar, honey, butter, oil, dairy, nuts, cocoa, salt, baking powder and others.

Which cup is in the recipe

There is not one cup. In US recipes a cup is 240 ml (the US customary cup is 236.6 ml), in metric recipes 250 ml, sometimes 200 ml, and in the UK an imperial cup is 284 ml. The difference between 200 and 250 ml is a quarter, so the calculator shows the volume in the name of every unit and lets you choose yours. For a 250 ml cup a cup of flour weighs ≈ 127 g, sugar ≈ 211 g, butter ≈ 239 g, water 250 g, honey ≈ 355 g and cocoa ≈ 97 g.

Spoons

A tablespoon is 15 ml (20 ml in Australia), a teaspoon is 5 ml and a dessert spoon 10 ml. The calculator uses level spoons, not heaped: a heaped spoon depends on the person and has no reliable standard. Examples for a level tablespoon: flour ≈ 7.6 g, sugar ≈ 12.7 g, honey ≈ 21 g, salt ≈ 18 g. For a teaspoon: sugar ≈ 4.2 g, baking powder ≈ 4 g, baking soda ≈ 6 g.

Fractions and easy input

Recipes often write amounts as fractions: 1/2 cup, 1 1/2 cups, ¾ spoon. In the calculator you can enter the amount exactly that way: 1/2, 1 1/2, 1-1/2, ½, 1½, as well as a decimal with a comma or a point. For example 1 1/2 cups of flour (250 ml) is about 190 g. For cups and spoons the answer also shows the nearest practical fraction, for example “about ½”.

How many grams in a cup

The “How many grams in…” tab shows the weight of ¼, ⅓, ½, ⅔, ¾, 1, 1½, 2 and 3 cups, and of spoons, for the chosen ingredient in grams, ounces and milliliters. There is also a reverse table: how many cups and tablespoons are in 25, 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1000 g. For example 100 g of flour is about 0.79 of a 250 ml cup. The “All ingredients” tab collects the weight of a cup, spoons and the density of all ingredients by category in one table.

US and UK units

For English-language recipes there are the ounce (28.35 g), the pound (453.6 g), the US fluid ounce (29.57 ml) and the UK one (28.41 ml), the US pint (473 ml) and the UK pint (568 ml) and the US quart (946 ml). The ounce and the pound are units of weight while the fluid ounce is a unit of volume: they are different things, and converting between them needs the density of the ingredient.

Where the densities come from and how accurate they are

For dry and thick ingredients (flour, sugar, grains, nuts, butter, sour cream) the weight of a cup is the median of values from several independent published charts (King Arthur Baking, Pastry by Saloni, Easy and Delish, Baker Bettie, Joy of Baking, Veg World and a Ukrainian chart for a 250 ml glass; US cup 236.6 ml), and for liquids (water, milk, oil, honey) the physical density is used. For 13 ingredients only one chart was found: their values are approximate, and the result says so. The real weight depends on packing, moisture and brand: for flour the difference between scooping and spooning reaches 10–15%, so the result is approximate (about ±5–10%). Other tables sometimes give different values, for example for buckwheat or rice, because grains pack differently.

Limits

The calculator does not include countable ingredients (an egg, an onion) because their size varies. For exact baking (dough, muffins, sponge cakes) weigh your ingredients on a kitchen scale and use the calculator for a quick estimate.

Ingredient densities are averages, so the result is approximate (about ±5–10%). For exact baking weigh your ingredients. A cup in a recipe may be 200, 250 or 240 ml: choose yours.

FAQ

How many grams are in a cup of flour?

A 250 ml cup holds about 127 g, a US cup (240 ml) about 122 g. The exact value depends on how the flour is packed.

How many grams of sugar are in a cup?

A 250 ml cup holds about 211 g of granulated sugar, a US cup (240 ml) about 203 g and a 200 ml cup about 169 g.

Which cup is used in Ukrainian recipes?

Most often 250 ml (a faceted glass), but 200 ml also occurs. If the recipe does not say, assume 250 ml and check by weight.

How does a US cup differ from an ordinary cup?

A US cup is 240 ml (legal) or 236.6 ml (customary), a metric cup is 250 ml, a British imperial cup 284 ml.

How many grams are in a tablespoon?

It depends on the ingredient: a level tablespoon (15 ml) of flour weighs about 7.6 g, sugar 12.7 g, honey 21 g, salt 18 g.

How many grams are in a teaspoon?

A level teaspoon (5 ml): sugar about 4.2 g, baking powder about 4 g, baking soda about 6 g, salt about 6 g.

How do I enter a fraction such as 1/2 cup?

Type 1/2, 1 1/2, ½ or 1½ in the “Amount” field: the calculator understands fractions, decimals with a comma or a point and symbols like ½ ⅓ ¼ ¾.

Why do tables differ between sources?

A cup can have a different volume, and an ingredient packs differently and varies in moisture. So the values are averages, about ±5–10%.

Are heaped spoons taken into account?

No, spoons are level. A heaped spoon depends on the person and has no reliable numeric standard.

Do I need to choose an ingredient to convert ml to liters?

No. Volume to volume and weight to weight do not depend on the ingredient, it is needed only to convert between volume and weight.

What is the difference between an ounce and a fluid ounce?

An ounce (28.35 g) measures weight, a fluid ounce (29.57 ml US, 28.41 ml UK) volume. Converting between them needs the density of the ingredient.

Is the converter accurate enough for baking?

It gives an approximate estimate (about ±5–10%). For exact baking weigh your ingredients on a kitchen scale.
