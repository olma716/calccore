What BMR and TDEE are

BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the calories the body burns at rest over a day: for breathing, circulation and organ function. TDEE (total daily energy expenditure) is the BMR multiplied by an activity factor, that is the real calories burned including movement and exercise. TDEE is the starting point for any goal: to lose weight you eat below it, to gain you eat above it, and to maintain you eat about as much.

Five basal-rate formulas

Mifflin–St Jeor (default): 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age, plus 5 for men or minus 161 for women. Harris–Benedict (revised, 1984): separate coefficients for men and women. Schofield (WHO): linear in weight, separately for ages 18–29, 30–59 and 60+. Katch–McArdle and Cunningham start from lean body mass (the body fat percentage is needed): 370 + 21.6 × lean mass and 500 + 22 × lean mass. For a 30-year-old man of 75 kg and 178 cm with 18% body fat they give 1718 (Mifflin), 1777 (Harris–Benedict), 1734 (Schofield), 1698 (Katch–McArdle) and 1853 kcal (Cunningham): a spread of about 9% around the mean of 1756 kcal.

How to choose the activity level

The multiplier scales the BMR: 1.2 — desk job and no exercise; 1.375 — light exercise 1–3 times a week; 1.55 — moderate 3–5 times; 1.725 — hard 6–7 times; 1.9 — physical work plus regular training. People most often overestimate their activity, so if in doubt choose the lower level and adjust by results. You can also enter your own multiplier from 1.0 to 2.5 if you know it. For the example above TDEE is 1717.5 × 1.55 ≈ 2662 kcal per day.

Calories for weight loss, maintenance and gain

The “Daily needs” tab shows five goals: fast loss (−20% of TDEE), moderate (−10%), maintenance, lean gain (+10%) and fast gain (+15%), plus the approximate weight change per week. For a TDEE of 2662 kcal that is 2130, 2396, 2662, 2928 and 3061 kcal, roughly −0.48, −0.24, 0, +0.24 and +0.36 kg per week. The calculation rests on a simplification: about 7700 kcal correspond to 1 kg of fat mass. If the calories fall below your BMR or below the commonly cited minimum of 1200 (women) or 1500 (men) kcal, the calculator warns you: this should not be done without medical supervision.

A plan for your goal weight

The “Weight goal” tab, from your current and goal weight and a pace (0.25–1 kg per week), computes the calories per day, the time and an estimated date. For example, losing from 75 to 68 kg at 0.5 kg per week needs a deficit of 550 kcal per day: with a TDEE of 2662 that is 2112 kcal and 14 weeks. The calculator warns if the pace exceeds about 1% of body weight per week, the deficit is over 25% of TDEE or the goal is further than two years away. The model is linear: in reality expenditure falls as weight drops, so the pace slows — recalculate after every 4–5 kg.

Exercise calories

The “Exercise calories” tab uses the MET — how many times an activity burns more energy than rest. Calories = MET × weight (kg) × hours. For running at 10 km/h (9.8 MET), a 70 kg person burns 343 kcal in 30 minutes of gross expenditure, 308 of them “extra” (net of rest). The MET values are averages from the physical activity compendium; the list has 23 activities: walking, running, cycling, swimming, strength work, yoga and team sports. The real expenditure depends on pace, terrain, technique and fitness.

Accuracy and limits

The formulas were derived on samples of healthy adults. The Mifflin–St Jeor formula was tested at about ages 19–78, Schofield for adults. For teenagers, pregnant women, people with metabolic disorders or significant overweight the result is only a rough guide. The best way to refine your needs is weeks of observation: track your weight and adjust the calories by the outcome. The result is not medical advice; with illness or significant weight loss consult a doctor or dietitian.

FAQ

What is BMR?

The basal metabolic rate is the calories the body burns at complete rest over a day. It is the minimum needed to sustain life, without movement.

What is TDEE?

Total daily energy expenditure: the BMR multiplied by an activity factor. It is the number of calories at which your weight stays the same.

Which BMR formula is the most accurate?

There is no single most accurate one. Mifflin–St Jeor most often has the smallest error for adults; Katch–McArdle is more accurate if the body fat percentage is known. The calculator shows the range of all formulas.

How do I calculate calories for weight loss?

Subtract a deficit from TDEE: 10–20% or 300–500 kcal a day. The “Weight goal” tab computes the deficit from a pace, for example 0.5 kg per week is 550 kcal a day.

Why 7700 kcal per kilogram?

It is a common approximation of the energy in 1 kg of fat mass. In reality the pace of weight change depends on body composition and varies over time, so it is a guide.

What is the minimum safe calorie intake?

A common guide without medical supervision is at least 1200 kcal for women and 1500 for men and not below your BMR. Individual needs may be higher.

What pace of weight loss is safe?

Usually 0.25–1 kg per week is advised, but no more than about 1% of body weight. A faster pace is more often accompanied by muscle loss.

How do I choose the activity level?

Consider your exercise and daily life: a desk job is 1.2, 3–5 workouts a week is 1.55. If in doubt choose the lower level and adjust by your weight after 2–3 weeks.

Why do the formulas give different results?

They were derived on different samples and have different shapes, and some account for body composition. A spread of a few percent is normal uncertainty of the estimate.

What is a MET?

The metabolic equivalent: how many times an activity burns more energy than rest (1 MET). Calories = MET × weight (kg) × hours.

Why do exercise calories differ between the compendium and a fitness tracker?

MET values are averages for a group of people, while trackers estimate from heart rate and motion. Both give a guide, not an exact measurement.

Does the calculator account for pregnancy or illness?

No. The formulas were derived on healthy adults, so in such cases the result is only a rough guide and your diet is better agreed with a doctor.
