What the roofing calculator counts

The calculator works out the slope area of a gable, hip (with an equal pitch) and shed roof, the ridge height, the lengths of the ridge, eaves, gable edges and hips for several buildings (up to six). The eaves overhang and the gable overhang are counted separately. Then, if you set the covering, the calculator finds the number of sheets or unit pieces with a reserve and, if you wish, trims, battens, membrane and the cost from your prices in hryvnia, dollars or euros.

How the slope area is counted

A slope has a length along the incline equal to the horizontal run to the ridge divided by the cosine of the pitch. A gable roof is two rectangles: the length with the gable overhangs by the length along the incline. A hip roof is two trapezoids and two triangles, and a shed roof is one rectangle. The sum of the slope areas is always the plan area with overhangs divided by the cosine of the pitch.

Example: a gable roof of a 10×8 m house with no overhangs at 30° gives two slopes of 4.62 m along the incline and an area of 92.4 m². A hip roof of 10×8 m with a 0.5 m overhang gives 114.3 m², a ridge of 2.0 m and hips of 6.87 m each. A shed roof of 6×4 m at 20° gives 25.5 m².

Sheets, unit covering and the reserve

For sheet covering the calculator counts columns of sheets by the base of every slope divided by the useful width of a sheet. If the length along the incline is more than the sheet length, the sheets are joined with an overlap in several rows. You can also choose sheets cut to the slope, then the number equals the number of columns. For unit covering the slope area with the reserve is divided by the useful area of one unit. The sheets over trapezoids and triangles are counted by the base, so this is a safe upper estimate.

Worked example

A hip roof of 10×8 m at 30° with a 0.5 m overhang and a gable roof of 6×5 m at 35° with an eaves overhang of 0.4 m and a gable overhang of 0.3 m. The area is 161.0 m². Sheets of 1.1×3 m with a 0.15 m overlap: 100 sheets, 105 with a 5% reserve. Trims of 2 m: 54 pieces, battens at a 35 cm step: 82 boards of 6 m, membrane with a 10% overlap: 3 rolls of 75 m². At 950 ₴ per sheet, 250 ₴ per trim, 180 ₴ per board and 1,900 ₴ per roll the cost is 133,710 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. Take the parameters of sheets, units and materials from your maker.

FAQ

How do I calculate the area of a roof?

The roof area is the plan area with overhangs divided by the cosine of the pitch. For a gable roof of 10×8 m with no overhangs at 30° that is 92.4 m².

Why is the roof area larger than the area of the house?

Because the slopes are inclined, and the area of an inclined surface is larger than its plan projection by a factor of 1/cos(pitch). The eaves and gable overhangs are added to that.

How do I calculate the ridge height?

The ridge height above the eaves line is the tangent of the pitch times half the width of the house (the whole width for a shed roof). For a house 8 m wide at 30° that is 2.31 m.

Does the calculator count a hip roof?

Yes, for a hip roof with an equal pitch on all slopes. The ridge is the difference between the length and the width, and the hips are counted separately.

How many sheets of metal tile or profiled sheet are needed?

The number of columns is the number of useful widths of a sheet that fit into the base of a slope, and the number of rows is how many sheets are needed along the slope with an overlap. You can also choose sheets cut to the slope.

What is the useful width of a sheet?

The width of a sheet after the overlap of the neighbouring sheets, given in the data of the maker. It is less than the full width of the sheet.

What reserve of covering should I take?

The reserve is set as a percentage and rounded up. It covers cutting, especially for hip roofs. The field is empty by default.

How are battens counted?

The rows of battens run at the given step from the eaves to the ridge, and the length of every row is the width of the slope at that height. The lengths are added and divided by the length of a board.

How much membrane is needed?

The roof area with an overlap in percent is divided by the area of a roll and rounded up.

What trims do I need for the ridge, eaves and edges?

The calculator counts the lengths of the ridge, eaves, gable edges and hips, and the number of trims is the length divided by the length of one trim, separately for each element.

Does the calculator account for valleys, windows and chimneys?

No. A building of a complex shape can be split into several roofs, and openings and waste covered by the reserve.

Which pitch should I choose?

It depends on the covering, the climate and the project, so you enter it in degrees. The calculator shows the ridge height for the chosen pitch.
