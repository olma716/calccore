What the Kepler's laws calculator does

The “Kepler's third law” tab links the orbital period T, the semi-major axis a and the mass M of the central body. “Elliptical orbit” finds the perihelion, aphelion, semi-minor axis, period and the speeds at the extreme points from the semi-major axis and the eccentricity, and the other way round. “Satellite orbit” works out a circular orbit around any body from the altitude or the period.

Kepler's first and second laws

First law: planets move along ellipses with the Sun at one focus. Second law: the line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, so a planet moves faster near perihelion and slower near aphelion: vₚ · q = vₐ · Q. For Halley's comet (a ≈ 17.834 AU, e ≈ 0.967) the perihelion is q = a(1 − e) ≈ 0.5885 AU and the aphelion Q = a(1 + e) ≈ 35.08 AU.

Kepler's third law

The squares of the orbital periods are in the ratio of the cubes of the semi-major axes: T² = a³ if T is in years and a in astronomical units (for bodies orbiting the Sun). For Mars a = 1.524 AU, so T = √(1.524³) ≈ 1.8814 years. In general T² = a³ / M, where M is the mass of the central body in solar masses; this is how planets and stars are weighed from the orbit of a companion.

Speed in orbit

The speed at any point of an elliptical orbit is given by the vis-viva equation: v² = GM · (2/r − 1/a). For a circular orbit (r = a) it reduces to v = √(GM / r). At an altitude of 420 km above the Earth (orbit radius 6791 km) the speed is about 7.66 km/s and the period T = 2πr / v ≈ 92.8 min. The escape speed at this altitude is √2 times larger.

Geostationary orbit

A satellite hangs above one point of the equator if its period equals one sidereal day (23.9345 h). From Kepler's third law the radius of such an orbit is about 42,164 km from the centre of the Earth, that is an altitude of about 35,793 km above the mean radius (35,786 km above the equator — the difference is due to the oblateness of the Earth). The speed there is about 3.07 km/s.

Examples of orbits

Earth: a = 1 AU, T = 1 year. Jupiter: a = 5.203 AU, T = √(5.203³) ≈ 11.87 years. Saturn: a = 9.537 AU, T ≈ 29.45 years. GPS navigation satellites fly at an altitude of about 20,200 km, the orbit radius is ≈ 26,571 km, the speed ≈ 3.87 km/s and the period ≈ 11.97 hours, that is half a day. For comparison, the ISS at an altitude of 420 km makes a revolution in 92.8 minutes. All these examples can be computed in the “Third law” and “Satellite orbit” tabs.

The masses, radii and orbits of the planets are approximate (NASA reference); orbits are circular and the mass of the satellite is neglected.

FAQ

What does Kepler's third law say?

The squares of the orbital periods of the planets are in the ratio of the cubes of the semi-major axes of their orbits: T² ∝ a³. For the Sun T² = a³ with T in years and a in AU.

How do I find the orbital period of a planet?

T = √(a³), with a in astronomical units and T in years. For Mars a = 1.524 AU gives T ≈ 1.88 years.

What are perihelion and aphelion?

The closest and the farthest points of the orbit from the Sun: q = a(1 − e) and Q = a(1 + e).

What is eccentricity?

A measure of how stretched an ellipse is: e = 0 is a circle, and the closer to 1, the more elongated the orbit. For the Earth e ≈ 0.017, for Halley's comet ≈ 0.967.

What is an astronomical unit?

The mean distance from the Earth to the Sun: 1 AU = 149,597,870,700 m.

How do I find the speed of a satellite?

v = √(GM / r), where r is the distance from the centre of the body (the radius of the body plus the altitude).

What is the speed of the ISS?

About 7.66 km/s, with an orbital period of about 92.8 minutes.

What is escape speed?

The speed needed to leave a body for good: √2 times the circular speed at the same distance.

Does the calculator take the mass of the planet into account?

In the third law you can set the mass of the central body. The mass of the satellite is taken as small and ignored.

Why does the geostationary altitude differ from 35,786 km?

The calculator measures from the mean radius of the Earth (6371 km), while the tabulated value is measured from the equatorial radius (6378 km).

Why do comets move faster near the Sun?

By Kepler's second law the line to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal times, so near perihelion, where the distance is small, the speed is larger; conservation of energy gives the same.

How long does a GPS satellite take to orbit?

About 11 hours 58 minutes — two revolutions a day, at an altitude of about 20,200 km.
