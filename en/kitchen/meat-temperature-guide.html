What the calculator does

The calculator shows the minimum safe internal temperature for meat, poultry, fish, eggs and leftovers, checks the reading of your thermometer and tells you when to take beef off the heat for the doneness you want. All temperatures are given in degrees Celsius and Fahrenheit.

USDA safe minimums

By the USDA FSIS chart all poultry (breasts, whole bird, thighs, ground, stuffing) must reach 165 °F (73.9 °C). Ground beef, pork, veal and lamb need 160 °F (71.1 °C). Steaks, chops and roasts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, and fresh ham, are done at 145 °F (62.8 °C) with a rest of at least 3 minutes. Fin fish needs 145 °F or until the flesh is opaque and separates with a fork. Egg dishes are cooked to 160 °F (71.1 °C), and leftovers and casseroles are reheated to 165 °F (73.9 °C).

Ham

For fully cooked ham that is reheated the value depends on its origin: ham packaged in a USDA-inspected plant is reheated to 140 °F (60 °C) and all other ham to 165 °F (73.9 °C). Fresh or smoked raw ham is cooked to 145 °F (62.8 °C) with a 3-minute rest.

Rest

USDA requires a rest of at least 3 minutes for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, lamb and fresh ham: during that time the temperature holds and pathogens die off. For poultry and ground meat USDA does not require a separate rest. A longer rest of large cuts is a matter of juiciness, not safety.

Doneness is not safety

Beef doneness levels (rare, medium rare, medium, medium well, well done) are flavor guides. According to ThermoWorks rare is served at 120–130 °F (49–54 °C), medium rare at 130–135 °F (54–57 °C), medium at 135–145 °F (57–63 °C), medium well at 145–155 °F (63–68 °C) and well done from 155 °F (68 °C). The first three levels are below the USDA minimum of 145 °F (63 °C) for whole cuts of beef, so the calculator shows a warning. Children, pregnant women, older adults and people with weakened immunity are better off choosing the safe minimum. Ground meat is always cooked to 160 °F.

Carryover and the pull temperature

After the meat is taken off the heat the temperature in the center keeps rising. The source advises allowing 3–5 °F (2–3 °C) for a thin steak and 10–15 °F (6–8 °C) for a large cut, and Thermapen gives similar pull temperatures. The calculator takes the middle of the range and subtracts it from the serving temperature: for a thin steak at medium rare that is 126–131 °F (52–55 °C). This is an estimate: carryover depends on thickness, the heat of the pan or oven and the accuracy of the thermometer.

How to measure

Insert the thermometer into the thickest part of the food, away from bones, fat and gristle. For poultry measure the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast. The color of the meat and juices does not show that it is safe, so go by the thermometer. For large cuts measure in several places.

Limits

The calculator does not replace the advice of a doctor or the health authorities. The temperatures in the table are minimums for ordinary foods, not a guarantee of safety: freshness, storage and hygiene matter too. Doneness levels are given for beef only because there is no reliable data on levels for other kinds.

FAQ

What temperature should chicken be cooked to?

To 165 °F (73.9 °C) in the thickest part, and for a thigh in the innermost part, not touching the bone.

What is the safe temperature for steak?

By USDA 145 °F (62.8 °C) with a rest of at least 3 minutes. Rare, medium rare and medium are below this minimum.

What temperature should ground meat be cooked to?

To 160 °F (71.1 °C) for beef, pork, veal and lamb and to 165 °F (73.9 °C) for ground poultry.

How hot should pork be?

For chops and roasts 145 °F (62.8 °C) with a rest of at least 3 minutes, for ground pork 160 °F (71.1 °C).

What is the temperature of cooked fish?

145 °F (62.8 °C) or until the flesh is opaque and separates easily with a fork.

How long should meat rest after cooking?

USDA requires at least 3 minutes for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal, lamb and fresh ham. For poultry and ground meat there is no separate rest.

What is carryover?

It is cooking from residual heat: the temperature in the center keeps rising by a few degrees after the meat leaves the heat (3–5 °F for a thin steak, 10–15 °F for a large cut).

When should I take a steak off the heat?

A few degrees before the target temperature: the calculator subtracts carryover from the serving temperature. For a thin steak at medium rare that is 126–131 °F (52–55 °C).

Is medium rare safe?

It is below the USDA minimum of 145 °F (62.8 °C) for whole cuts of beef. Children, pregnant women, older adults and people with weakened immunity are better off choosing the safe minimum.

What temperature should ham be heated to?

Fully cooked ham from a USDA-inspected plant to 140 °F (60 °C), other ham to 165 °F (73.9 °C), and fresh or smoked raw ham is cooked to 145 °F (62.8 °C).

Can I go by the color of the meat?

No, the color of meat and juices does not show that it is safe. Measure with a thermometer in the thickest part.

What temperature should leftovers be reheated to?

To 165 °F (73.9 °C), and the same for casseroles.
