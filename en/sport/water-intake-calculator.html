How much water should you drink daily

Exact water needs depend on body weight, physical activity level, climate, and overall health. The basic formula is approximately 30-35 ml of water per kg of body weight per day, with additional adjustments for exercise and heat.

Why water matters

  • Thermoregulation: water helps your body cool down through sweating.
  • Nutrient transport: blood, which is 90% water, delivers oxygen and nutrients to cells.
  • Joint function: water is part of synovial fluid, which lubricates joints.
  • Athletic performance: even 2% dehydration can significantly reduce endurance and strength.

Factors affecting water needs

  • Physical activity: exercise increases fluid loss through sweat, so you need more water.
  • Climate: hot weather increases sweating even without exercise.
  • Diet: a high-protein diet and caffeine can slightly increase water needs.

This is an estimate. Individual needs may vary depending on health conditions (for example, kidney or heart disease requires a different approach to fluid intake) — consult a doctor for specific medical conditions.

FAQ

How much water should I drink per day?

The basic recommendation is about 30-35 ml of water per kg of body weight, adjusted for activity level and climate.

Does coffee and tea count toward daily water intake?

Yes, all fluids, including coffee and tea, count toward total fluid intake, though caffeine has a mild diuretic effect.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes, excessive water intake can lead to hyponatremia (dilution of salts in the blood). This is a rare condition, but it's important not to significantly exceed your needs without reason.
