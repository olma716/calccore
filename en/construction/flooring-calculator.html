What the flooring calculator counts

The calculator works out how much flooring you need for one or several rooms (up to six). For laminate, vinyl and parquet board the result is whole packs with a reserve, for linoleum and carpet it is running metres of the roll cut in strips, with seams and a cutting plan. You can also add an underlay with an overlap, skirting that accounts for doors and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices. Tile has its own calculator.

How packs are counted

The net area of all rooms (after subtracting bays and niches) is multiplied by the reserve and divided by the area one pack covers, rounded up. For two rooms of 5.5 × 4.2 m and 6 × 3 m the net area is 41.10 m², with a 10% reserve 45.21 m², at 2.22 m² per pack that is 21 packs (46.62 m²). The reserve depends on the layout: you set it (for example 10%), and a diagonal layout needs more than a straight one.

Linoleum and carpet: cutting in strips

A roll is cut in strips along the full length of the room, so the metres do not equal the area divided by the roll width. For every room the calculator compares two directions of the strips. The number of strips is the side to cover divided by the roll width (rounded up), the length of each strip is the side along the strip plus a trim allowance on each side. The direction with the shorter length is chosen. For a pattern repeat, the repeat length is added to every strip after the first.

The rooms are cut separately and the leftovers of one room are not used in another. For rooms of 5.5 × 3.2 m and 3 × 2 m with a 3.5 m roll and a 5 cm trim that is 5.6 m and 2.1 m, 7.7 m in total (26.95 m² of the roll for a net area of 23.60 m²).

Underlay and skirting

The underlay is counted from the net area with an overlap (10% by default), in rolls if you give the roll area. The skirting is counted from the perimeter of the rooms minus the doors (a default width of 0.9 m) and divided by the length of a piece, rounded up. For the laminate example: 45.2 m² of underlay (5 rolls of 10 m²), 35.6 m of skirting (15 pieces of 2.5 m). The defaults can be edited.

Worked example

For laminate in two rooms with a 10% reserve: 21 packs, 5 rolls of underlay and 15 pieces of skirting. At 1,500 ₴ per pack, 250 ₴ per underlay roll, 90 ₴ per piece of skirting and 500 ₴ delivery the cost is 34,600 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real consumption depends on the layout, the pattern and the cutting.

FAQ

How much laminate do I need for a room?

The net area of the room plus the reserve, divided by the area of a pack, rounded up. For example, for 41.10 m² with a 10% reserve and a 2.22 m² pack that is 21 packs.

What reserve of laminate should I allow?

You set the reserve (for example 10%); an empty field means 0%. A diagonal layout and complex rooms need more than a straight one: various sources give 15–20% for a diagonal.

How do I count linoleum for a room?

Work out how many strips you need: the side to cover divided by the roll width, rounded up. The length of every strip is the length of the room plus a trim allowance. The calculator does it for both directions and picks the shorter one.

Why can't linoleum be counted as the area divided by the roll width?

Because the roll is cut in strips along the full length. For a 3 × 5 m room and a 4 m roll, the area divided by the width gives 3.75 m, but a strip of 5.10 m is really needed (the length of the room with a trim allowance).

What roll width does linoleum come in?

The most common widths are 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 and 4 m. If the roll is at least as wide as the side to cover, no seams are needed: for a 5.5 × 3.2 m room a 3.5 m roll works with one 5.6 m strip.

How many seams will linoleum have?

The number of seams is one less than the number of strips. For a 5.5 × 4.2 m room and a 3 m roll that is 2 strips and 1 seam. The calculator shows the seams in the result and on the cutting plan.

What is a pattern repeat and how does it affect the count?

A repeat is how often the pattern repeats. For the pattern to match at the seam, every strip after the first needs extra length equal to the repeat. For a 5.5 × 4.2 m room and a 2 m roll that is 13.5 m with a 30 cm repeat against 12.9 m without it.

How do I count carpet?

The same way as linoleum: pick “Linoleum, carpet (roll)” and the roll width, enter the length and width of each room. The result is in running metres.

How much underlay for laminate?

The net area with an overlap (10% by default), in rolls if you give the roll area. For 41.10 m² that is 45.2 m², or 5 rolls of 10 m².

How do I count skirting?

The perimeter of the rooms minus the width of the doors, divided by the length of a piece, rounded up. For the two-room example the perimeter is 37.4 m, minus two doors of 0.9 m, that is 35.6 m, or 15 pieces of 2.5 m.

Can I count several rooms together?

Yes, add up to six rooms. For packs the areas are summed, for a roll every room is cut separately.
