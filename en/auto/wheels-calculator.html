How the tire and wheel size calculator works

The calculator computes the overall wheel diameter: the rim diameter (inches × 25.4 mm) plus two sidewall heights, where the sidewall is tire width × profile ÷ 100. For 205/55 R16 the sidewall is 112.8 mm, the wheel diameter is 631.9 mm, the circumference is 1.985 m, which is about 504 revolutions per kilometre.

When two sizes are compared it shows the diameter difference in percent, the ground clearance change (half of the diameter difference), the real speed at 50, 90 and 130 km/h on the speedometer and the odometer error. For 205/55 R16 → 225/45 R17 the diameter changes by +0.38%, the ground clearance by +1.2 mm, and at 100 km/h on the speedometer the real speed is 100.4 km/h.

How to read a tire size

In 205/55 R16 the number 205 is the tire width in millimetres, 55 is the sidewall height as a percentage of the width, R stands for radial construction and 16 is the rim diameter in inches. You can paste a size as one line into the width field — it splits into three fields automatically.

What diameter difference is acceptable

A common rule is to keep the diameter difference within ±2%. Up to 3% usually still works, but check the permitted sizes in the car manual or with a dealer. Over 3% is not recommended: the speedometer and odometer become noticeably inaccurate, and ABS/ESP, the gearbox and ground clearance may behave differently. These are general guidelines, not a requirement of a specific manufacturer.

Rim width and offset (ET)

Each tire has an approved range of rim widths. For 205/55 R16 it is roughly 5.5–7.5″ (the measuring rim is about 6.0″). A wider rim stretches the tire and a narrower one squeezes it: about 10.2 mm of tire width per inch of difference.

The ET offset is the distance from the wheel’s mounting face to its centreline. A smaller ET pushes the wheel out, a larger one tucks it in. Going from 205/55 R16 (6.5J, ET45) to 225/45 R17 (7.5J, ET42) moves the outer edge of the tire out by 13 mm and the inner edge closer to the suspension by 7 mm — check this on the car. The calculator does not check the bolt pattern (PCD) or the centre bore.

The result is an estimate from nominal sizes: the real diameter depends on the brand, tread wear, pressure and load. Always check rim width and ET against the tire maker’s table and the car documents.

FAQ

What wheel diameter difference is acceptable?

A common rule is no more than ±2%. Up to 3% usually still works, but check the permitted sizes in the car manual. Over 3% is not recommended because of the speedometer error and possible ABS/ESP issues.

Why does the speedometer reading change?

The speedometer is tied to the wheel circumference. If it is larger, the car travels farther per revolution and the real speed is higher than shown. For 205/55 R16 → 225/45 R17, 100 km/h on the dial is actually 100.4 km/h. Under UNECE R39 a speedometer must not show less than the real speed, so large deviations in that direction are undesirable.

How do I calculate the real speed and distance?

Real speed = speedometer reading × new circumference ÷ old circumference. The odometer is off in the same way: for 205/55 R16 → 225/45 R17, 100 km on the odometer is 100.4 km actually driven.

Can I fit a wider tire on the same rim?

Yes, if the rim width is within the tire’s approved range. For 205/55 R16 it is roughly 5.5–7.5″. The wider the rim compared with the measuring rim, the wider the tire on it: about 10.2 mm per inch. At the edge of the range the tire is stretched or squeezed, and the manufacturer may not allow such a fitment.

What is offset (ET) and how does it affect the fitment?

ET is the distance in millimetres from the wheel’s mounting face to its centreline. A smaller offset pushes the wheel out (closer to the arch), a larger one tucks it in (closer to the suspension). Together with the tire width this changes the position of the outer and inner edges of the tire; the calculator shows both shifts.

How does ground clearance change with the size?

The ground clearance changes by half of the diameter difference. For 205/55 R16 → 225/45 R18 the diameter grows by 27.8 mm, so the clearance increases by about 13.9 mm. This is wheel geometry only; suspension height and sag under load are not taken into account.

What are revolutions per kilometre and why do they matter?

It is the number of wheel revolutions per 1 km: 1,000,000 mm ÷ circumference. For 205/55 R16 it is about 504. It is needed to recalibrate the speedometer or a tuning module after a size change.

Does tire size affect fuel consumption?

Indirectly, yes: wider and heavier tires and wheels increase rolling resistance and mass, and a diameter change alters the effective gear ratio. The odometer also starts counting kilometres differently, which distorts consumption figures based on the dashboard. You can estimate trip costs in the fuel calculator.

Should I check the load and speed index?

Yes. The load index (LI) and speed index (SI) of the new tire must not be lower than those specified by the car maker. The calculator does not check this.

How do I paste a size as one line?

Paste a size such as 205/55 R16 (205/55-16 or 205/55R16 also work) into the width field: width, profile and rim diameter are filled in automatically.
