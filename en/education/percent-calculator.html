What the percentage calculator works out

The percentage calculator is a tool for quick calculations with percentages. It has four modes: “X% of a number”, “What %?”, “± by %” and “Difference %”. Enter the numbers in the fields and the result appears at once with the formula and an example of the substitution. The calculator is free, works in the browser and needs no sign-up.

X% of a number

To find a percent of a number, multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 15% of 2000 is 2000 × 15 / 100 = 300. This is how discounts, tips, taxes and a share of a budget are worked out. The “X% of a number” mode does this automatically and shows the substitution.

What percent a part is

To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 120 of 800 is 120 / 800 × 100 = 15%. The “What %?” mode helps to judge the completion of a plan, a share of costs or a grade by the number of correct answers. The whole cannot be zero.

Increasing and decreasing by a percent

To increase a number by a percent, multiply it by (1 + p / 100), and to decrease it, by (1 − p / 100). For example, 200 + 15% is 230 and 200 − 15% is 170. This is how the price after a markup or a discount is worked out. The “± by %” mode shows both the new number and the size of the change.

Percentage difference

To compare an old and a new value, divide the difference by the old value and multiply by 100: (new − old) / old × 100. From 80 to 100 the growth is 25%, while from 100 to 80 the drop is 20%. A percentage change depends on the base number, so it is not symmetric. The old value cannot be zero.

VAT, discounts and markups

For a VAT of 20% the price with tax is the price without it times 1.2, and the price without VAT is the price with VAT divided by 1.2, not minus 20%. A 30% discount on an item at 1500 is 450 and the item costs 1050. For such tasks use the “X% of a number” and “± by %” modes.

A percent and a percentage point

A percentage point is the difference between two percentages: if a rate rose from 10% to 12%, it rose by 2 percentage points but by 20% relative to the original. The calculator works out the relative change in the “Difference %” mode, and the difference of the percentages is simple subtraction.

Limits

The calculator accepts numbers with a comma or a period as the decimal separator and spaces between digits. A result below 1000 is shown with up to six decimal places and from 1000 it is rounded to two. Nothing is sent anywhere: everything is worked out in your browser. For loans and deposits, where interest builds up over time, use the matching calculators.

An increase by a percent followed by a decrease by the same percent does not bring back the original number: +20% and then −20% of 100 give 96, not 100. A percentage is always taken of its own “before”.

FAQ

How do I calculate X% of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20% of 500 = 500 × 20 / 100 = 100. The calculator does this automatically in the “X% of a number” mode.

How do I find what percent one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 120 of 800 is 15%. Use the “What %?” mode.

How do I increase a number by a percent?

Multiply the number by (1 + p / 100). If you add 15% to 200, you get 200 × 1.15 = 230.

How do I decrease a number by a percent?

Multiply the number by (1 − p / 100). If you subtract 15% from 200, you get 200 × 0.85 = 170. This is how the price after a discount is worked out.

How do I calculate a discount in percent?

Find the percent of the price and subtract it: a 30% discount on 1500 is 450 and the price after the discount is 1050. Or multiply by 0.7 straight away.

How do I work out a price with or without VAT?

For a rate of 20% the price with VAT is the price without VAT × 1.2 and the price without VAT is the price with VAT / 1.2. You cannot subtract 20% from the price with VAT.

Why do +20% and −20% not cancel each other out?

Because each percentage is taken of its own number. 100 plus 20% gives 120 and 20% of 120 is 24, so the result is 96.

How do I work out the change in percent between two numbers?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old and multiply by 100. From 80 to 100 it is +25%, from 100 to 80 it is −20%. Use the “Difference %” mode.

How is a percent different from a percentage point?

A percentage point is the difference of two percentages: from 10% to 12% is +2 points, while the relative change is +20%.

Can I enter numbers with a comma?

Yes, both a comma and a period work as the decimal separator, and spaces between digits are allowed.

To how many places is the result rounded?

A result below 1000 is shown with up to six decimal places and from 1000 it is rounded to two.

Are my numbers stored?

No, everything is worked out in your browser and nothing is sent or stored.
