What running pace is

Running pace is the time it takes you to run one kilometre (or mile), for example 5:30 per km. For planning training and races it is handier than speed: a pace of 5:30/km equals 10.9 km/h, and in miles it is 8:51 per mile (6.8 mph). One mile is 1.609344 km and the calculator shows both.

How to find pace, time and distance

The three values are linked by time = distance × pace. On the first tab choose what to find and enter the other two. For example, 10 km at 5:30/km is 55:00; 10 km in 55:00 gives a pace of 5:30/km; and 55:00 at 5:30/km is exactly 10 km. Alongside, the calculator builds a table of times over standard distances at this pace: 5 km in 27:30, a half marathon (21.0975 km) in 1:56:02, a marathon (42.195 km) in 3:52:04. Seconds are rounded correctly, with no “5:60”.

Splits: even and negative pace

The “Splits” tab lays out your target time for every kilometre or mile with the running total. For a half marathon in 1:45:00 the average pace is 4:59/km. If the second half is 2% faster (a negative split), the first half runs at 5:02/km, the second at 4:56/km and you are at 53:02 at halfway. Zero in “faster by” gives an even pace. A negative split usually helps you avoid burning out at the start.

Result prediction with the Riegel formula

In 1977 the engineer Peter Riegel proposed the formula T₂ = T₁ · (D₂ / D₁)^1.06: the time for a new distance depends on the time for a known one. The exponent 1.06 means you slow down by about 6% each time the distance doubles. For 5 km in 25:00 the formula gives 10 km in 52:07, a half marathon in 1:55:00 and a marathon in 3:59:47 (5:41/km). A prediction is most reliable for distances not much longer than yours; a marathon from a 5 km result usually comes out too optimistic because it needs specific endurance. You can change the exponent if you know your tendency towards long or short distances.

VDOT and Daniels training paces

Coach Jack Daniels proposed rating fitness with a number called VDOT: it comes from the speed of your race and the share of maximal oxygen uptake you can hold for that time. Training paces are calculated from VDOT: easy (about 59–74% of VO₂max), marathon, threshold (≈ 88%), interval (≈ 98%) and repetition (≈ 105%). For 5 km in 20:00 you get a VDOT of 49.8: easy running 4:54–5:53/km, marathon pace 4:32, threshold 4:16, interval 3:55 and repetition 3:42 per km. Equivalent results: 10 km in 41:28, a half marathon in 1:31:50, a marathon in 3:11:17. It is a guide that can differ from the official tables by a few seconds.

Using paces in training

Most of the weekly volume is run at an easy pace where you can talk freely. Threshold pace (usually 20–40 minutes continuously or in repeats) builds the ability to hold speed for long, interval pace develops maximal oxygen uptake and repetition pace develops speed and running economy. To control the load, combine pace with heart rate: there is a heart rate zone calculator for that.

Limits of the calculation

All formulas are statistical models. Terrain, heat, wind, altitude, shoes and health can change the real time by minutes. The Daniels formula is more accurate for results from a few minutes to about three hours. Beginners and people with chronic conditions should agree training loads with a doctor.

This is a formula-based calculation for planning: terrain, weather, fitness and how you feel affect the real time. See a doctor or coach before hard training and races.

FAQ

How do I calculate running pace?

Divide the time by the distance. For example 10 km in 55:00 is 55:00 / 10 = 5:30 per kilometre.

How do I calculate finish time?

Multiply pace by distance. For a pace of 5:30/km and 10 km it is 55:00, for a half marathon (21.0975 km) it is 1:56:02.

How is pace different from speed?

Pace is minutes per kilometre and speed is kilometres per hour. A pace of 5:30/km is a speed of 10.9 km/h. The calculator shows both.

How do I convert pace from km to miles?

Multiply pace by 1.609344: 5:30/km is 8:51 per mile. And the other way, 8:00 per mile is 4:58 per kilometre.

How long is a half marathon and a marathon?

A half marathon is 21.0975 km and a marathon is 42.195 km. The calculator uses exactly these values.

What is a negative split?

Running the second half faster than the first. The calculator lets you set by what percent the second half is faster and lays out the time for each kilometre.

What is the Riegel formula?

An empirical formula T₂ = T₁ · (D₂ / D₁)^1.06 for predicting time over another distance from a known result. The exponent 1.06 can be changed.

Is a marathon prediction from a 5 km result accurate?

No, it is usually too optimistic: a marathon needs specific endurance. It is more reliable to predict distances not much longer than the known one.

What is VDOT?

A running fitness score by Jack Daniels that depends on the distance and time of your race. Training paces and equivalent results are calculated from it.

Which training paces does the calculator compute?

Easy, marathon, threshold, interval and repetition, in minutes per kilometre and per mile.

Which result should I enter for VDOT?

A recent race or an all-out time trial over a distance from 1500 m to a marathon. An old or half-hearted result gives an understated VDOT.

Does the calculator account for terrain and weather?

No. It calculates by formulas for a flat route in normal conditions; heat, hills and wind will increase the real time.
