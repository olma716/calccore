What the tile calculator counts

The calculator works out the number of tiles for floors and walls (up to six surfaces): each has a length and a width or height, or a ready area, plus the openings area. The tile size, the joint and the reserve are shared. The result is the tiles with a reserve, packs, the area bought and what is left over and, if you wish, adhesive, grout and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How the number of tiles is counted

The area is divided by the area of one tile together with the joint: (length + joint) × (width + joint), all in metres. The reserve is added and the number is rounded up. For a 300×300 mm tile that is 11.11 pcs per 1 m² without a joint and 10.89 pcs with a 3 mm joint, 2.0% less, so it is worth entering the joint. For a 600×300 mm tile it is 5.56 pcs per 1 m² without a joint and 5.47 pcs with a 3 mm joint.

Packs are counted by pieces (the tiles divided by the pieces per pack, rounded up) or by m² (the tile area divided by the m² per pack). The reserve for cutting depends on the layout: you set it (for example 10%), and a diagonal or complex layout needs more than a straight one. The “Layout” list only fills a suggestion into the reserve field (straight and offset 10%, diagonal 15%, herringbone 20%), which you can change.

Adhesive and grout

The adhesive consumption depends on the notch of the trowel. According to the Ceresit CM 11 Pro data sheet: a 6 mm notch ≈ 2.7 kg/m² (tiles up to 15 cm), 8 mm ≈ 3.6 kg/m² (up to 25 cm), 10 mm ≈ 4.2 kg/m² (up to 30 cm). The real consumption depends on the evenness of the base and the brand, so you can enter your own. The adhesive is counted for the net area and the bags are rounded up.

The grout is counted by the formula: (length + width) / (length × width) × tile thickness × joint width × density (dimensions in mm, density in kg/dm³, the result in kg/m²). For a 300×300 mm tile, 8 mm thick, a 2 mm joint and a density of 1.6 that is 0.17 kg/m². The density of a cement grout is 1.6–1.72 in the sources and can be changed.

Worked example

A floor of 3.2 × 2.4 m (7.68 m²), a 600×300 mm tile, a 3 mm joint and a 10% reserve: 42.0 tiles without the reserve, 47 pcs with it. At 8 pcs per pack that is 6 packs (48 pcs, 8.64 m²), 1 pc left over. Adhesive with an 8 mm notch: 27.6 kg, which is 2 bags of 25 kg. Grout for a 9 mm tile: 1.66 kg, 1 bag of 2 kg. At 650 ₴ per m², 380 ₴ per bag of adhesive, 150 ₴ per bag of grout and 300 ₴ delivery the cost is 6,826 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real consumption depends on the layout, the cutting and the evenness of the base.

FAQ

How many tiles per 1 m²?

It depends on the size and the joint. For a 300×300 mm tile it is 11.11 pcs without a joint and 10.89 pcs with a 3 mm joint, for 600×300 mm 5.56 and 5.47 pcs.

How do I calculate the number of tiles for a room?

The surface area is divided by the area of the tile with the joint, the reserve is added and the number is rounded up. For a 7.68 m² room and a 600×300 mm tile with a 3 mm joint and a 10% reserve that is 47 pcs.

Why account for the joint width?

The joints increase the area one tile takes. For a 300×300 mm tile with a 3 mm joint, ignoring the joint would overstate the count by 2.0%, and by more for smaller tiles.

What reserve of tiles should I allow?

You set the reserve (for example 10%); an empty field means 0%. Various sources give about 10% for a straight layout, 15% for a diagonal one and 20% or more for a complex pattern.

How does the reserve depend on the layout?

A diagonal layout and complex patterns (herringbone) leave more waste because the tiles are cut at the edges more often. Various sources give about 10% for a straight layout, 15% for a diagonal one and 20% or more for a complex pattern. The “Layout” list fills these values into the reserve field, and you set the exact percentage.

How do I count packs of tiles?

Enter how many tiles are in a pack, or how many m². By pieces the tiles are divided by the pieces per pack, by m² the tile area is divided by the m² per pack, both rounded up. For 47 pcs and 8 pcs per pack that is 6 packs.

How much adhesive per 1 m² of tiles?

According to the Ceresit CM 11 Pro data sheet a 6 mm notch gives about 2.7 kg/m², 8 mm about 3.6 kg/m², 10 mm about 4.2 kg/m². Shops quote higher values, so you can enter your own consumption.

How do I choose the trowel notch?

In the Ceresit CM 11 Pro data sheet a 6 mm notch is for tiles up to 15 cm, 8 mm up to 25 cm, 10 mm up to 30 cm. Bigger tiles and an uneven base need a bigger notch, and the bag of your adhesive has the final say.

How much grout per 1 m²?

By the formula (L + W) / (L × W) × thickness × joint × density. For a 300×300 mm tile, 8 mm thick, a 2 mm joint that is 0.17 kg/m²; for the example 600×300 mm, 9 mm, a 3 mm joint it is 0.216 kg/m².

How do I account for windows and doors on walls?

Pick the type “Wall (L × H)” and enter the openings area in m²: it is subtracted from the wall area.

How do I count tiles for a floor and walls together?

Add every surface separately: the floor, the walls, the backsplash. The calculator sums the tiles, adhesive and grout of all the surfaces for the shared tile size.

Should I buy tiles from one batch?

Yes, it is better to buy the whole amount at once: tiles of different batches can differ a little in shade, and the size (calibre) may not match.
