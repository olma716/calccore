What the insulation calculator counts

The calculator works out how much insulation is needed for a structure to meet the requirement for the thermal resistance R. For every item (walls, roof, attic, floor or your own requirement, up to six) it shows the required and the actual thickness, the layers of plates, the achieved R, and the total number of plates and packs with a reserve and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How the thickness is counted

The thermal resistance of a layer is its thickness divided by the thermal conductivity λ. The insulation has to add to the existing structure what is missing to the requirement: R of the insulation = R required − R existing, thickness = R of the insulation × λ. The number of layers of plates is taken to reach the thickness, so the actual thickness is a multiple of the plate thickness and the achieved R is a little above the requirement. The resistance of the existing structure is the sum of d/λ of its layers (for a 0.38 m layer with λ 0.7 it is 0.54); if it is unknown, leave the field empty.

An example for a wall with no existing resistance and mineral wool of λ 0.040: for a requirement of 3.3 (the old edition) 13.2 cm are needed, for 3.5 (zone II) 14 cm, for 4.0 (zone I) 16 cm.

Requirements of DBN V.2.6-31:2021

Since 1 September 2022 DBN V.2.6-31:2021 “Thermal insulation and energy efficiency of buildings” has been in force. The minimum thermal resistance of the external walls of residential and public buildings is 4.0 m²·K/W for temperature zone I and 3.5 for zone II (it was 3.3 and 2.8 in the 2016 edition). The values for roofs (combined 7.0 / 6.0, attic 6.0 / 5.5) are from secondary sources, and for floors you have to enter them yourself. The requirements can be edited, and for new construction the standard allows a reduction to 80% if the other energy efficiency indicators are met. Check against the current text of the standard.

Worked example

Walls of 120 m² with 18 m² of openings (102 m² of insulation), a requirement of 3.5, an existing structure of 0.6: the insulation needs 2.9, which is 116 mm, and with 50 mm plates that is 3 layers, 150 mm, R = 4.35. A roof of 80 m², a requirement of 6.0, an existing 0.3: 228 mm, 5 layers, 250 mm, R = 6.55. The volume is 35.3 m³, 1,177 plates (1000×600 mm), 1,236 plates with a 5% reserve, which at 12 plates per pack is 103 packs. At 650 ₴ per pack the cost is 66,950 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate by a simplified model. A project needs a thermal engineering calculation.

FAQ

How do I calculate the thickness of insulation?

The thickness = (R required − R of the existing structure) × λ of the insulation. For a wall with a requirement of 3.5, an existing 0.6 and mineral wool of λ 0.040 that is 116 mm.

What is thermal resistance R?

It is the ability of a structure to resist the flow of heat, in m²·K/W. For a layer R = thickness (m) / λ, for a structure R is the sum of the layers. The higher the R, the warmer the structure.

What thermal resistance of walls is required by DBN?

By DBN V.2.6-31:2021 the minimum for the external walls of residential and public buildings is 4.0 m²·K/W in temperature zone I and 3.5 in zone II. That is more than 3.3 and 2.8 in the 2016 edition.

Which temperature zone should I pick?

The zone depends on the location of the building according to DBN. The calculator suggests the requirement for the chosen zone; if you are not sure, check the zone of your city in the text of the standard.

Why does a roof need a thicker insulation?

Because the standards require a higher thermal resistance for roofs than for walls: a combined roof 7.0 / 6.0 against 4.0 / 3.5 for walls (the roof values are from secondary sources).

What is the existing R and where do I get it?

The resistance of the structure without insulation: the sum of d/λ of its layers. For example, a 0.38 m layer with λ 0.7 gives R = 0.54. If you do not know it, leave it empty: the insulation then covers the whole requirement, which gives a margin.

Why is the actual thickness larger than the required one?

The plates are sold in a fixed thickness. The calculator rounds the number of layers up: for the required 116 mm with 50 mm plates that is 3 layers, 150 mm.

What λ should I use for the insulation?

The one from the data sheet of your product. The sources give 0.035–0.045 for mineral wool, 0.032–0.040 for EPS, 0.028–0.035 for XPS, 0.022–0.028 for PIR, in W/(m·K). The calculator suggests the middle values. The design λ in the standards is a little higher than the declared one.

How much insulation for 100 m² of wall?

At a requirement of 3.5 with no existing resistance and mineral wool of λ 0.040 the thickness is 14 cm, which is 14.0 m³ per 100 m².

How do I count packs of insulation?

The number of plates (area × layers / plate area) with the reserve is divided by the plates per pack and rounded up. For the example 1,236 plates and 12 per pack is 103 packs.

Does the calculator account for thermal bridges?

No. The calculator counts in a simplified way with one layer of insulation. Thermal bridges, fixings and moisture affect the real resistance, so a project needs a thermal engineering calculation.

Can I lower the requirement for new construction?

For new construction the standard allows lowering the reduced resistance to 80% of the minimum if the other energy efficiency indicators are met. In the calculator change the “Required R” field by hand.
