What the paint calculator counts

The calculator works out how much paint you need for walls and ceilings: litres with a reserve and the number of cans. You can add up to six surfaces: the walls of a room by dimensions, a single wall, a ceiling or a ready area, with openings. The paint, the number of coats and the reserve are shared by all the surfaces. If you wish, primer and the cost from your prices are added. There are no built-in prices.

How the paint is counted

Litres are the area multiplied by the number of coats and divided by the coverage (m² per 1 L per coat), and the reserve is added to the result. Cans are rounded up. The area of the walls of a room is the perimeter multiplied by the height, minus the openings; the ceiling is length × width.

The coverage is taken from the label. For interior acrylic and water-based paints the sources give 8–12 m² per 1 L per coat, for facade paints 5–9 m²/L. The calculator suggests the middle values of 10 and 7, which you can change. For 20 m² in one coat that is 2.50 L at 8 m²/L and 1.67 L at 12 m²/L.

Cans and the leftover

If you enter the can volume, the calculator shows the number of cans and draws them: the full ones and the last one filled by a fraction. The leftover is the difference between the litres bought and the litres needed. For the example of a room with 48.6 m² of walls (with the openings) and a ceiling that is 13.8 L, 3 cans of 5 L, the last one filled to 75%, with 1.2 L left over.

Worked example

A room of 5 × 4 m, 2.7 m high, openings of 6 m², and a ceiling of 5 × 4 m: walls 42.6 m², ceiling 20 m², 62.6 m² in total. In 2 coats at 10 m²/L that is 12.5 L, 13.8 L with a 10% reserve, which is 3 cans of 5 L. Primer in one coat at 10 m²/L: 6.9 L, 2 cans of 5 L. At 1,300 ₴ per can of paint and 600 ₴ per can of primer the cost is 5,100 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real consumption depends on the paint, the base and the way it is applied.

FAQ

How much paint per 1 m²?

It depends on the paint: for interior acrylic and water-based paints the sources give 8–12 m² per 1 L per coat, which is 0.08–0.125 L/m². For two coats the litres are doubled. The exact coverage is always printed on the label.

How do I calculate the paint for a room?

Multiply the perimeter of the walls by the height, subtract the windows and doors, add the ceiling if needed, multiply by the number of coats and divide by the coverage. For a 4 × 3 m room, 2.5 m high, with 5 m² of openings the area of the walls is 30.0 m².

How many coats of paint are needed?

For interior work usually two. Three or more are needed when going from a dark colour to a light one or to cover stains.

Should the ceiling be counted with the walls?

Add it as a separate surface “Ceiling (L × W)”. The paint and the number of coats are then shared. If the ceiling gets a different paint, count it in a separate run.

How do I convert L/m² to m²/L?

Divide 1 by the value: 0.1 L/m² is 10 m²/L, 0.125 L/m² is 8 m²/L. The calculator works in m² per 1 L, as printed on the cans.

How many cans of paint should I buy?

The litres with the reserve are divided by the can volume and rounded up. For 13.8 L and a 5 L can that is 3 cans (15 L), with 1.2 L left over.

What reserve of paint should I allow?

You set the reserve (for example 10%); an empty field means 0%. The sources give 10–15% for an uneven base and touch-ups, and more for a porous base.

Is a primer needed and how much?

A primer reduces the amount of paint the base soaks up. Take its coverage from the label: enter the m² per 1 L and the number of coats, and the calculator counts the litres and cans for the same area.

Why does more paint go on site?

A porous or rough surface (for example fresh plaster) soaks up more paint, and the way it is applied matters too. Then enter a smaller coverage per m² or a bigger reserve.

How do I account for windows and doors?

Enter the total openings area in m² for each surface: it is subtracted. Reveals, if they are painted, add as a separate “Area” surface.

How much paint for a facade?

For facade paints the sources give 5–9 m² per 1 L per coat. Pick “Facade” or enter your own coverage from the can.

How do I calculate the cost of paint?

Enter the price per litre or per can (a can needs its volume). The cost = the quantity × the price + delivery. For the example 3 cans at 1,300 ₴ and 2 cans of primer at 600 ₴ give 5,100 ₴.
