What the calculator shows

The calculator finds the Moon phase on any date from 1900 to 2100: the name of the phase, the illumination, the age of the Moon (how many days have passed since the New Moon) and the angle between the Moon and the Sun. It draws the Moon with the correct terminator and shows the exact moments of the nearest New Moon, First Quarter, Full Moon and Last Quarter in UTC. For example, the Full Moon of January 25, 2024 happened at 17:54 UTC.

Exact moments, not an average cycle

The Moon completes a cycle from New Moon to New Moon in 29.53 days on average, but the real cycle varies from 29.27 to 29.83 days, so a calculation with the average gives an error of up to half a day. The calculator works out the real moments of the phases with astronomical formulas and takes the phase on a date from the true position of the Moon and the Sun. The main phases are accurate to within a few minutes and the illumination to about ±0.5%.

Illumination and the age of the Moon

The illumination depends on the angle between the Moon and the Sun: it equals (1 − cos angle) / 2. At the New Moon the angle is 0° and the illumination is near zero, at the First Quarter it is 90° and 50%, at the Full Moon 180° and 100%, at the Last Quarter 270° and again 50%. The age of the Moon is the time since the previous New Moon. For example, on January 25, 2024 at 19:00 UTC the Moon is 14.3 days old and the angle is 180.5° (a Full Moon). On November 6, 2026 at midday only 8% is lit, a Waning Crescent, and the next Full Moon arrives on November 24.

Eight phases

There are four main phases: New Moon, First Quarter, Full Moon and Last Quarter. Between them the phase is named by the angle: Waxing Crescent and Waxing Gibbous before the Full Moon, Waning Gibbous and Waning Crescent after it. “Quarter” means a quarter of the cycle, while half of the disc is lit. Between two neighbouring main phases there are 6.6 to 8.2 days. The day on which the exact moment of a main phase falls is marked with that phase.

The northern and southern hemispheres

In the northern hemisphere a waxing Moon is lit on the right and a waning one on the left. In the southern hemisphere, for example in Australia or Brazil, everything is mirrored. That is why the calculator has a hemisphere choice: the picture and the emoji change accordingly, while the moments of the phases are the same for everyone.

The calendar for a month and the phases for a year

The “Calendar for a month” tab shows for each day the phase and the illumination at midday and marks the main phases with their exact time. The “Phases for a year” tab collects all the New Moons, quarters and Full Moons of the year by month. In 2026 there are 13 Full Moons, and in May there are two: May 1 and 31. The second Full Moon in a calendar month is called a “Blue Moon”: it is only a name, the Moon does not change color.

The Moon and the Earth

The Moon causes tides in the oceans. The strongest, spring tides, occur near the New Moon and Full Moon, when the Sun, Earth and Moon are almost in a line. For observing faint objects the best time is near the New Moon, while a Full Moon lights up the sky. Folk traditions (planting, fishing, haircuts) based on the lunar calendar have no scientific support, so the calculator gives no such advice.

Limits

Dates from 1900 to 2100 are allowed. The Moon picture is schematic: a diagram of the phase, not a photograph. The calculator does not show moonrise and moonset or the distance of the Moon (that needs the place of observation), and it does not tell a “supermoon”.

FAQ

What is today's Moon phase?

Open the “Phase on a date” tab: today's date is set by default and the calculator shows the phase, the illumination and the age of the Moon.

How long does the lunar cycle last?

The mean length of the synodic month is 29.53 days, and the real one varies from 29.27 to 29.83 days. From one main phase to the next there are 6.6 to 8.2 days.

How is the illumination of the Moon determined?

By the angle between the Moon and the Sun: the illumination equals (1 − cos angle) / 2. It is near zero at the New Moon, 50% at the quarters and 100% at the Full Moon.

What is the age of the Moon?

It is the time since the previous New Moon. The Full Moon arrives at about 14 to 15 days.

How do I find the exact moment of a Full Moon or New Moon?

The calculator shows the nearest New Moon, First Quarter, Full Moon and Last Quarter with the exact time in UTC, and the “Phases for a year” tab collects them for the whole year.

What is a Blue Moon?

The second Full Moon in a calendar month. It is only a name, the Moon does not change color. In 2026 there is one in May: May 1 and 31.

How many Full Moons are there in a year?

Usually 12 and sometimes 13. In 2026 there are 13.

Why does the Moon look different in the two hemispheres?

In the northern hemisphere a waxing Moon is lit on the right, in the southern one on the left. The hemisphere choice mirrors the picture.

How do the phases differ from an average calculation?

An average cycle gives an error of up to half a day, while the calculator works out the real moments with astronomical formulas to within a few minutes.

Does the calculator account for the time zone?

The moments and the input time are in UTC. The phase on a date is taken at noon UTC, and if you give a time, at that time.

Does the calculator give planting or haircut advice by the Moon?

No: such traditions have no scientific support. The calculator only shows astronomical data.

Which years does the calculator support?

From 1900 to 2100. It does not calculate moonrise and moonset or a “supermoon”.
