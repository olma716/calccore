What the generator does

The generator creates random passwords, passphrases, PINs, usernames and nicknames. On the “Password” tab choose a length from 4 to 128 characters, the types of characters (lowercase and uppercase letters, digits, symbols) and a symbol set, exclude look-alike characters (0, O, 1, l, I) if you need to and require at least one character of each type. The result comes with the entropy in bits, the strength and an approximate cracking time.

Why the randomness is secure

Characters are chosen with crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's cryptographic generator rather than the ordinary Math.random. To make every character equally likely, values from the incomplete tail of the range are rejected, so there is no modulo bias that appears when you take a remainder. The “each type” requirement is met by rejecting a whole password, which keeps the distribution uniform among the valid ones.

What entropy is

Entropy in bits shows how many guesses it takes to find a password: each bit doubles the number of possibilities. For a random password it equals the length times log₂ of the pool size. A 16-character password from a pool of 88 symbols gives over 100 bits. The calculator uses the real pool after exclusions and counts the “each type” requirement exactly with the inclusion–exclusion formula.

A passphrase

A phrase of words is easier to remember than a string of characters. The words come from the short EFF list of 1296 words, so each adds about 10.3 bits: 5 words give about 51.7 bits, 6 words 62 and 7 words over 72. For accounts 5–6 words are usually enough, and for the master password of a manager 7 or more are better. The words are chosen at random, not invented by you.

A PIN and the limit on attempts

A PIN has few possibilities: 4 digits give only 10,000 combinations, or about 13.3 bits. It is protected not by complexity but by a limit on attempts: an ATM or a phone locks after a few mistakes. The generator can remove obvious patterns (111, 123, 321 in a row), counts the remaining options exactly and shows the chance of guessing in three tries.

Checking your own password

On the “Check” tab enter a password and the calculator shows the entropy by the formula and a conservative estimate: repeats, sequences (abc, 123) and keyboard rows (qwerty) count as one or two characters, and a resemblance to a common password lowers the score sharply. The check works only in your browser and does not compare the password with leak databases, so do not enter a password you use today.

Usernames and nicknames

Usernames are built from a first and last name (Cyrillic is transliterated by the Ukrainian 2010 rules: oleh.makhnovets, o.makhnovets, makhnovets_oleh) or from random words (quiet_river42). Nicknames are made of an adjective and a noun from our own word lists on the themes of animals, nature, space, fantasy, history and technology. You can choose the separator, digits, maximum length and style.

Limits and safety

The cracking time is only an approximation under assumed speeds: real ones depend on how a service stores the password. The check cannot see dictionary words or personal data. The main risks are phishing, a database leak and reusing a password, and no generator solves them: use a unique password for every site, a password manager and two-factor authentication.

The best password is long, unique for every site and kept in a password manager. Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it is offered: it protects you even if the password leaks.

FAQ

Is it safe to generate a password on a website?

Here it is: the password is created in your browser and is not sent anywhere or put in the link, browser storage or analytics. Still, do not use passwords created on other sites unless you are sure they work the same way.

How long should a password be?

For accounts usually 14–16 random characters, for a password manager and important services 20 or more. Length matters more than complexity: each extra character multiplies the number of possibilities.

What is password entropy?

It is a measure of unpredictability in bits: each bit doubles the number of possibilities. For a random password it equals the length times log₂ of the character pool size.

What makes this generator better than an ordinary one?

It uses a cryptographic generator without modulo bias, guarantees characters of each chosen type without losing uniformity, works out the real entropy after exclusions and shows the cracking time.

Why exclude look-alike characters?

The characters 0, O, 1, l and I are easy to confuse when a password is copied by hand. Excluding them slightly shrinks the pool and the entropy, and the calculator accounts for that.

Why is a passphrase better than a string of characters?

It is easier to remember and type at the same strength. 6 random words give about 62 bits, roughly like 10 random characters from a 62-character pool.

Can I make up the phrase myself?

Better not: people choose predictable words and quotes. A phrase protects only when the words are chosen at random, as the generator does.

What makes a PIN safe?

A random one, without birth dates or obvious patterns, and a different one for each card or device. A PIN depends on a limit on attempts: without it any PIN is guessed in seconds.

Does the calculator check whether my password has leaked?

No. It does not use the network and does not compare the password with leak databases. You can check leaks on dedicated services, but do not enter a password you use today.

How does the calculator rate my password?

It works out the entropy from the length and character pool and a conservative estimate where repeats, sequences and keyboard rows count as one or two characters and common passwords lower the score sharply.

Where does the cracking time come from?

It is half of all possibilities divided by an assumed guess rate: 100 an hour for an online sign-in, 10 billion a second for a fast PC and a trillion a second for a GPU farm. It is only a guide.

Can I use the generated usernames and nicknames?

Yes, but check that they are free on the service you need. Do not put a password or private data in them.
