How the calculator works

The calculator computes two monthly totals. An own car is fixed costs (insurance, parking, loan, depreciation) plus variable costs that depend on mileage (fuel or electricity and service). A taxi is the number of trips × the price of one trip (the base fare, a fixed charge for boarding, plus the price per km, but not less than the minimum fare). For car sharing the price of a trip is minutes × price per minute + kilometres × price per km.

In the example: 40 trips of 8 km, 8 l/100 km, fuel at 55 UAH/l. The fixed costs of the car are 5,800 UAH a month, the variable ones 47.2 UAH per trip. The own car comes to 7,688 UAH a month, a taxi with a 60 UAH boarding fee and 12 UAH per km to 6,240 UAH (156 UAH per trip).

The break-even point

The break-even point is the number of trips per month at which an own car and a taxi cost the same: fixed costs ÷ (taxi price per trip − variable cost of the own car per trip). In the example it is 54 trips, about 432 km a month: below that mileage a taxi is cheaper, above it your own car is.

If the example grows to 80 trips, the own car costs 9,576 UAH versus 12,480 UAH for a taxi.

What is included in the own-car cost

The calculator takes into account fuel or electricity, service and tires per kilometre, insurance, parking, a loan or lease, depreciation and other fixed costs. Fines, repairs after an accident and time spent at the service are not included.

The result is an estimate from the numbers you enter. Time, comfort and waiting are not priced in, and a car’s depreciation is an assumption you set.

FAQ

What is cheaper: your own car or a taxi?

It depends on mileage. An own car has large fixed costs that do not depend on trips, so at low mileage a taxi is cheaper and at high mileage your own car is. In the example the border is about 54 trips a month.

How is the break-even point calculated?

The monthly fixed costs of the car are divided by the difference between the price of a taxi (or car sharing) trip and the variable cost of the own car per trip. That is the number of trips from which your own car is cheaper. If a taxi trip costs less than the fuel and wear of your own car, a taxi is cheaper at any mileage.

Which car costs does the calculator include?

Fuel or electricity, service and tires per kilometre, insurance, parking, a loan or lease, depreciation and other fixed costs. Anything you leave empty counts as zero.

How do I account for the car’s depreciation?

Enter the car price and the yearly depreciation percentage: the calculator divides the amount by 12 months. For example, for a 600,000 UAH car and 10% a year it is 5,000 UAH a month. The percentage is an assumption: for most cars it is 8–15% a year, so enter your own resale forecast.

How is the taxi price calculated?

The base fare (a fixed boarding charge) plus the price per km × distance, but not less than the minimum fare. For a 60 UAH boarding fee, 12 UAH per km and an 8 km trip it is 156 UAH. Tariffs differ between services and change with demand, so enter your real prices.

How is car sharing calculated?

The price per minute × the trip duration + the price per km × distance. For 5 UAH per minute, 20 minutes and 4 UAH per km over 8 km it is 132 UAH per trip, which is 5,280 UAH a month with 40 trips.

Why is an own car usually better with an electric vehicle?

Electricity per kilometre is usually much cheaper than fuel. If in the example you switch to an electric motor (17 kWh/100 km, 8 UAH/kWh), the variable cost drops to 22.9 UAH per trip and the break-even point moves to 44 trips. You can estimate charging tariffs in the EV charging cost calculator.

Are travel time and waiting taken into account?

No. The calculator compares money only. Waiting for a taxi, looking for parking and comfort may matter, but everyone values them differently.

Does the calculator include parking for a taxi or car sharing?

Parking is not needed for a taxi or car sharing, so it is not counted for them. For your own car, parking is entered separately as a monthly amount.

How accurate are the results?

It is an estimate from the numbers you enter. The more exactly you enter the real fuel consumption, the tariffs and the fixed costs, the closer the result.
