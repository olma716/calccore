How much weight to gain during pregnancy

The recommended gain depends on your pre-pregnancy BMI (weight in kg divided by height in meters squared). The calculator uses the ranges of the US Institute of Medicine (IOM, 2009), which doctors around the world rely on. For example, a height of 5 ft 6 in and a weight of 137 lb give a BMI of 22.1 — normal weight, so the recommended gain for the whole pregnancy is 25–35 lb (11.5–16 kg).

  • Underweight (BMI < 18.5): 12.5–18 kg (28–40 lb)
  • Normal weight (BMI 18.5–24.9): 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb)
  • Overweight (BMI 25–29.9): 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb)
  • Obesity (BMI ≥ 30): 5–9 kg (11–20 lb)

For twins the ranges are higher: 17–25 kg (37–54 lb) at a normal BMI, 14–23 kg (31–50 lb) if overweight and 11–19 kg (25–42 lb) with obesity. For underweight women expecting twins the IOM gives no official range. For triplets and more the IOM has published no recommendations either, so the calculator shows only your gain, the BMI and a reference figure without a range.

How the weekly range is built

Weight gain is uneven. In the first trimester, up to week 13, women usually gain only 1.1–4.4 lb (0.5–2 kg): the baby is still tiny and morning sickness often gets in the way of eating. After that the gain becomes nearly steady, about 1 lb a week at a normal BMI, and reaches the total limit at week 40.

So the calculator builds the range between two lines: the lower one goes from 1.1 lb at week 13 to the lower limit of the range at week 40, the upper one from 4.4 lb to the upper limit. In the example (BMI 22.1) the range at week 24 is 10.8–16.9 lb and a gain of 14 lb fits into it. 11–21 lb remain until week 40, that is 0.69–1.31 lb a week.

According to the IOM, the recommended rate in the second and third trimesters is 0.44–0.58 kg a week if underweight, 0.35–0.50 at a normal weight, 0.23–0.33 if overweight and 0.17–0.27 with obesity (about 1–1.3, 0.8–1.1, 0.5–0.7 and 0.4–0.6 lb).

The calculator is a reference based on the IOM recommendations, not a medical assessment. The individual pace of weight gain is set by the doctor who looks after your pregnancy.

FAQ

How much weight should I gain during pregnancy?

It depends on your pre-pregnancy BMI: 12.5–18 kg (28–40 lb) if underweight, 11.5–16 kg (25–35 lb) at a normal weight, 7–11.5 kg (15–25 lb) if overweight and 5–9 kg (11–20 lb) with obesity (one baby, IOM).

How much weight is gained in the first trimester?

Usually 1.1–4.4 lb (0.5–2 kg) up to week 13, whatever the BMI. Morning sickness can even lower the weight, and that is not rare.

How much can I gain per week?

In the second and third trimesters about 0.35–0.50 kg (0.8–1.1 lb) a week at a normal BMI, 0.44–0.58 kg if underweight, 0.23–0.33 kg if overweight and 0.17–0.27 kg with obesity.

What gain is normal at week 24?

For a BMI of 22.1 (5 ft 6 in, 137 lb) the range at week 24 is 10.8–16.9 lb. A gain of 14 lb fits into it and 11–21 lb remain until week 40.

How do I calculate my pre-pregnancy BMI?

Divide the weight in kg by the height in meters squared: 62 / (1.68²) ≈ 22.0. Categories: under 18.5 — underweight, 18.5–24.9 — normal, 25–29.9 — overweight, 30 and above — obesity.

Which weight counts as the pre-pregnancy weight?

Your weight before conception or measured early in pregnancy. If you do not have an exact figure, use the weight from your first doctor’s visit.

What if my gain is above or below the range?

The range is a guide, not a diagnosis: temporary deviations happen. If it persists for several weeks, tell your doctor. Do not restrict your diet on your own, and do not try to “catch up” with the norm.

How much weight should I gain with twins?

By the IOM provisional ranges: 17–25 kg (37–54 lb) at a normal BMI, 14–23 kg (31–50 lb) if overweight and 11–19 kg (25–42 lb) with obesity. For underweight women there is no range, the target is set by the doctor.

How much weight should I gain with triplets?

There is no official IOM range for triplets. Clinical sources usually name about 50 lb (23 kg) for the whole pregnancy, roughly 1.5 lb (0.7 kg) a week, but these figures rest on one small study. The target gain is set by your doctor.
