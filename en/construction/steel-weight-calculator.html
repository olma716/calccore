What the metal weight calculator counts

The calculator works out the weight of rolled metal for several items (up to six): round bar, hexagon, square, flat bar, sheet, pipes, angle, channel and I-beam. The material is picked from a list (steel, stainless, cast iron, aluminium, copper, brass, bronze, titanium) or you enter your own density. The result is the weight by item and in total in kilograms and tonnes, with a reserve and, if you wish, the cost from your price. There are no built-in prices.

How the weight is counted

The weight of 1 m of a profile is the cross-section area multiplied by the density of the material, and the weight of an item is kg/m × length × quantity. The cross-section area: a circle π·d²/4, a hexagon √3/2·S², a square a², a flat bar width × thickness, a pipe π/4·(D² − (D − 2t)²), a rectangular tube A·B − (A − 2t)(B − 2t), an angle t·(A + B − t). For a sheet the weight is width × length × thickness × density.

For steel (7,850 kg/m³) that gives, for example, 0.888 kg/m for a Ø12 mm round bar and 2.466 kg/m for Ø20 mm. A 3 mm sheet weighs 23.6 kg/m² in steel and 8.1 kg/m² in aluminium.

Channel and I-beam: the sortament

A channel and an I-beam have tapered flanges and radii, so counting them by geometry is not accurate. The calculator takes kg/m from the sortament by number: channel GOST 8240-97 (series U) and I-beam GOST 8239-89. For example, a 12U channel is 10.43 kg/m, a 20 I-beam is 21.0 kg/m. For another material the mass is scaled by density/7,850. If the profile is non-standard, pick “Custom kg/m”.

Worked example

Steel: a Ø57×3 mm pipe, 10 pcs of 6 m, a 12U channel, 4 pcs of 6 m, and 2 sheets of 1250×2500×3 mm. The pipe is 3.995 kg/m, 239.7 kg; the channel 250.3 kg; the sheets 147.2 kg (73.6 kg each). In total 637.2 kg, with a 5% reserve 669.1 kg, or 0.669 t. At 38 ₴ per kg the cost is 25,425 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real weight depends on the tolerances, the radii and the coating.

FAQ

How do I calculate the weight of metal?

Multiply the cross-section area in mm² by the density and the length: the weight of 1 m = the area × the density. For a sheet the weight = width × length × thickness × density. For steel the density is 7,850 kg/m³.

What is the density of steel?

For carbon steel 7,850 kg/m³ is used. For stainless about 7900 (the sources give 7700–8000 depending on the grade), for cast iron about 7200.

How much does a metre of round bar weigh?

By the formula π·d²/4 × density: for Ø12 mm 0.888 kg/m, for Ø20 mm 2.466 kg/m (steel 7,850 kg/m³). There is a separate calculator for rebar.

How much do a square, a hexagon and a flat bar weigh?

For steel: a 20×20 mm square 3.14 kg/m, a hexagon of 20 mm across flats 2.72 kg/m, a 40×4 mm flat bar 1.26 kg/m.

How much does a square metre of sheet weigh?

The thickness in metres × the density. For a 3 mm steel sheet that is 23.6 kg/m², for aluminium 8.1 kg/m². A 1250×2500×3 mm steel sheet weighs 73.6 kg.

How do I calculate the weight of a pipe?

The cross-section area is the difference of the circles: π/4 × (D² − (D − 2t)²), where D is the outer diameter and t the wall. For a Ø57×3 mm pipe that is 3.995 kg/m in steel.

How do I calculate a rectangular tube and an angle?

Rectangular tube: A·B − (A − 2t)(B − 2t), angle: t·(A + B − t). This ignores the radii of the corners, so the weight is slightly overstated. For example, a 50×50×5 mm angle comes to 3.73 kg/m and a 40×40×3 mm tube to 3.49 kg/m. Check the sortament for accuracy.

Where does the weight of a channel and an I-beam come from?

From the sortament: channel GOST 8240-97 (series U) from 5U to 40U, I-beam GOST 8239-89 from 10 to 60. The reference masses are counted from the nominal dimensions for steel at 7,850 kg/m³, and the real mass differs within the tolerance (+3/−5% in the source).

How do I calculate the weight of aluminium, copper or stainless?

Pick the material from the list: the calculator takes its density. For example, a Ø20 mm round bar in aluminium is 0.848 kg/m against 2.466 kg/m in steel.

What is the percentage reserve for?

The reserve is added to the total weight for cutting, waste and errors. You set it (for example 5%), an empty field means 0%. For the example that is 637.2 kg without it and 669.1 kg with it.

Why does the real weight differ from the calculated one?

Because of the tolerances on the dimensions, the radii, the coating and the grade of the metal. A calculation from the nominal dimensions gives an approximate weight.

How do I convert kilograms to tonnes and work out the cost?

A tonne is 1000 kg. Enter the price per kg or per tonne, the cost = the weight with the reserve × the price + delivery. For the example 0.669 t.
