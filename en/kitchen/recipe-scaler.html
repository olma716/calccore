Why you need a recipe scaler

Recipes are often written for a specific number of servings that may not match your situation — for example, a recipe for 4 people when you have 8 guests, or the opposite — you need to cook less for one person. Manually recalculating every ingredient takes time and is easy to get wrong.

How the scaling works

The calculator computes a scaling factor as the ratio of desired servings to original servings (for example, 6 servings instead of 4 = a 1.5x factor), then multiplies each ingredient amount by that factor.

What you should still adjust manually

  • Spices and seasonings: when significantly scaling up a recipe, it's better to add spices gradually and taste rather than scale them exactly proportionally.
  • Leavening agents (baking soda/powder): in large quantities they can add a bitter taste — it's recommended to slightly reduce the proportion when significantly scaling up.
  • Cooking/baking time: doesn't scale linearly! Doubling a recipe doesn't mean doubling the oven time — a larger pan or more food requires separate doneness checks.
  • Baking pan size: when changing the number of servings, you'll likely need to change the pan size too.

For best results, write ingredients in the format "amount + unit + name" (e.g. "2 cups flour") so the calculator can correctly detect the number to scale.

FAQ

How do I use the recipe scaler?

Enter each ingredient with its amount on a separate line, specify the original and desired number of servings — the calculator will automatically scale all amounts proportionally.

Do all ingredients scale the same way?

The calculator scales all amounts proportionally, but note: spices, salt, leavening agents, and cooking time don't always scale linearly — adjust them to taste.

Can I enter fractional numbers?

Yes, you can enter both whole numbers and decimals (e.g., 1.5 cups).
