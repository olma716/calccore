Julian and Gregorian calendars: the difference

The Julian calendar (introduced by Julius Caesar in 45 BC) counts every fourth year as a leap year, so its average year is 365.25 days. That is slightly longer than the tropical year, so the calendar slowly drifted from the seasons. In 1582 Pope Gregory XIII introduced the Gregorian calendar, in which centuries not divisible by 400 are not leap years: the average year is 365.2425 days. The gap was already 10 days then, and after 4 October 1582 came 15 October at once.

Why the gap is 13 days now

Each century that is a leap year in the Julian calendar but not in the Gregorian one adds one more day to the gap: 1700, 1800, 1900, 2100, 2200 and so on. So in the 18th century the gap was 11 days, in the 19th 12, in the 20th and 21st 13 days, and from 1 March 2100 (Julian) it will be 14. That is why Christmas on 25 December of the Julian calendar falls on 7 January in the new style, and the Old New Year on 14 January.

How the conversion works

The calculator first converts your date to the Julian day number (JDN) — a continuous day count shared by both calendars — and then restores the date in the other calendar. This avoids accumulating errors at century boundaries. 25 December 2025 in the Julian calendar becomes 7 January 2026, a Wednesday; 1 March 1900 in the old style is 14 March in the new one; and 29 February 1900 (a day that does not exist in the Gregorian calendar but does in the Julian) is 13 March.

Fixed feasts of the Julian calendar

Orthodox feasts with a fixed date — Christmas (25 Dec), Epiphany (6 Jan), Presentation (2 Feb), Annunciation (25 Mar), John the Baptist (24 Jun), Peter and Paul (29 Jun), Transfiguration (6 Aug), Dormition (15 Aug), Pokrova (1 Oct), Entry into the Temple (4 Dec), Saint Nicholas (6 Dec) — stay on the same Julian dates. On the Gregorian calendar in 2026 they fall on 7 Jan, 19 Jan, 15 Feb, 7 Apr, 7 Jul, 12 Jul, 19 Aug, 28 Aug, 14 Oct, 17 Dec and 19 Dec. To find the Gregorian date of any feast in any year, enter its Julian date in the converter. Since 2023 the OCU and some parishes celebrate the fixed feasts by the Revised Julian calendar: its dates coincide with the Gregorian ones, so Christmas is on 25 December.

When countries switched

Countries moved to the Gregorian calendar at different times: Catholic Europe in 1582, Great Britain and its colonies in 1752 (2 September was followed by 14 September), the territory of the former Russian Empire in early 1918 and Greece in 1923. That is why historical dates often have a double form “O.S. / N.S.”, for example the October Revolution — 25 October Old Style and 7 November New Style. In the converter you can enter a date in either calendar and see its counterpart at once.

Special cases: BC and before 1582

For dates before 15 October 1582 the Gregorian calendar is proleptic — extended backwards by the modern rules although it was not in use then. Years BC are entered with the separate “Era” field: there is no year zero, so 1 BC is followed directly by AD 1. Internally the calculator uses astronomical numbering where 1 BC is year 0. The Revised Julian calendar, used by some churches for the fixed feasts, coincides with the Gregorian one until 2800, so the feasts fall on the same dates as in the Gregorian calendar. Not all churches and parishes have switched, so check the dates against the calendar of your community.

For dates before 15 October 1582 the Gregorian calendar is proleptic (extended backwards by the modern rules). Real switches to the new style happened at different times in different countries.

FAQ

How do I convert a date from the old style to the new?

Choose “Julian” as the source calendar and enter the day, month and year — the Gregorian date appears at once. For the current century add 13 days.

Why is the gap between the calendars 13 days?

Because every century not divisible by 400 (1700, 1800, 1900) is a leap year in the Julian calendar but not in the Gregorian, and each added one more day to the initial 10.

When will the gap become 14 days?

From 1 March 2100 in the Julian calendar, that is 15 March 2100 in the Gregorian: 2100 is not a leap year in the new style.

When is Christmas in the Julian calendar in 2026?

25 December 2025 in the old style is 7 January 2026 (a Wednesday) in the new style.

What is the Old New Year?

The New Year of the Julian calendar: 1 January Old Style falls on 14 January in the new style.

What is the Julian day number (JDN)?

A serial number of a day in a continuous count. It is shared by all calendars, so it is convenient for converting between them: 1 January 2000 in the Gregorian calendar is JDN 2,451,545.

Why did 10 days disappear in 1582?

Pope Gregory XIII removed the accumulated gap: Thursday 4 October 1582 was followed by Friday 15 October. The weekdays did not shift.

Is there a 29 February 1900?

In the Julian calendar yes, in the Gregorian no. So 29.02.1900 Old Style is 13.03.1900 New Style.

How do I enter dates BC?

Choose “BC” in the “Era” field and enter the year. There is no year zero: 1 BC directly precedes AD 1.

Does the weekday depend on the calendar?

No, the day is the same, so the weekday is identical in both calendars: 25.12.2025 Old Style and 7.01.2026 New Style are both Wednesday.

What is a proleptic calendar?

A calendar extended to dates before its introduction. The Gregorian calendar before 15 October 1582 is proleptic, so dates then might differ between countries.

Does the calculator include the Revised Julian calendar?

Not separately: the converter works with the Julian and Gregorian calendars. The Revised Julian coincides with the Gregorian until 2800, so for modern dates its dates are the same as the Gregorian ones (Christmas by it is 25 December).
