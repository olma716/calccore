What the gravity calculator does

The “Weight on planets” tab shows how much your body would weigh on another celestial body and gives a table for all bodies at once. The “g, v₂ and density” tab computes the surface gravity, the orbital and escape speeds and the density from the mass and radius; it also works backwards — finding the mass or the radius.

Mass and weight are different

The mass of a body is the amount of matter in it; it is the same everywhere (kg). The weight is the force with which the body presses on its support: P = m · g (N), and it depends on the gravitational acceleration g. For a 70 kg body on Mars (g = 3.73 m/s²) P = 70 · 3.73 = 261.1 N. A spring scale calibrated on the Earth would show 26.6 kg, about 38 % of the Earth weight.

Surface gravity

At the surface of a body of mass M and radius R: g = G · M / R², where G = 6.6743·10⁻¹¹ m³/(kg·s²). For Mars (M = 6.4171·10²³ kg, R = 3389.5 km) we get g ≈ 3.73 m/s². The table values come from a reference, and the one computed from the mean radius may differ slightly — especially for the gas giants, whose “surface” is conventionally taken at the 1-bar level.

Escape and orbital speeds

The first cosmic (orbital) speed is the circular speed near the surface: v₁ = √(GM / R); the second (escape speed) is v₂ = √2 · v₁. For Mars v₁ ≈ 3.55 km/s and v₂ ≈ 5.03 km/s (for the Earth 7.9 and 11.2 km/s). This is why Mars and the Moon hold only a thin atmosphere.

Mean density

The density of a body is ρ = 3M / (4πR³). For Mars ρ ≈ 3934 kg/m³ (3.93 g/cm³) — a rocky planet. Saturn has a density lower than water (≈ 0.69 g/cm³) because it consists mostly of hydrogen and helium.

Gravitational acceleration on the bodies of the Solar System

Mercury 3.70 m/s², Venus 8.87, Earth 9.81, the Moon 1.62, Mars 3.73, Jupiter 24.79, Saturn 10.44, Uranus 8.87, Neptune 11.15, Pluto 0.62 m/s². On the Sun g ≈ 274 m/s². Surface gravity depends on both the mass and the radius: Saturn is 95 times more massive than the Earth, but because of its large radius its surface gravity is only about 6 % greater.

The data of the bodies (mass, radius, g) come from the NASA reference, rounded. The weights in the table are at the equator; for the gas giants at the 1-bar level.

FAQ

How much do I weigh on the Moon?

The Moon has g = 1.62 m/s², about 1/6 of the Earth's, so a 70 kg body weighs about 113 N there and a spring scale would show about 11.6 kg.

Does the mass of a body change on other planets?

No. The mass is the same everywhere; only the weight changes because the gravitational acceleration changes.

Which planet has the strongest gravity?

Among the planets, Jupiter: g ≈ 24.8 m/s² (at the 1-bar level). On the Sun g ≈ 274 m/s².

Why do the gas giants have no surface?

They have no solid surface. A level is conventionally taken where the pressure equals 1 bar (close to the atmospheric pressure of the Earth).

How do I find the gravitational acceleration on a planet?

g = G · M / R², where M is the mass and R the radius of the planet.

What is the first cosmic speed?

The minimum horizontal speed at which a body becomes a satellite and does not fall to the surface: v₁ = √(GM / R). For the Earth about 7.9 km/s.

What is the second cosmic speed?

The speed needed to leave a body for good: v₂ = √2 · v₁. For the Earth about 11.2 km/s.

Why does the computed g differ slightly from the tabulated one?

The tabulated value is at the equator with rotation and oblateness taken into account, while the formula GM/R² gives the value for a sphere with the mean radius.

Which units can I use?

Mass: kg, M⊕, M☉, Jupiter and Moon masses. Radius: m, km, R⊕, R☉ and others. Results can be shown in convenient units.

Can I find the mass of a planet?

Yes: if g and the radius are known, M = g · R² / G.

Do astronauts in orbit feel weightless even though gravity is there?

Yes: at the altitude of the ISS the Earth's gravity is about 88 % of the surface value, but the station and the astronauts are in free fall, so there is no support and the weight disappears.

Why is g on Saturn almost like on Earth?

Saturn is 95 times more massive than the Earth, but its radius is about 9 times larger, and g is proportional to M / R², so the value ends up close to the Earth's.
