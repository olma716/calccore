What the solution calculator does

Six tabs cover typical solution chemistry problems. “Mass fraction” links the mass of the solute, solvent, solution and the fraction. “Molarity” finds c, n, V or the mass; the molar mass can be entered as a formula. “Dilution” and “Mixing” solve preparation problems, “ω ↔ mol/L” converts percentages to moles per litre, and the last tab computes molality and normality.

Mass fraction of the solute

The mass fraction is ω = m₁ / m, where m₁ is the mass of the solute and m = m₁ + m₂ is the mass of the solution. For 20 g of salt and 180 g of water the solution weighs 200 g and ω = 20 / 200 = 0.1, that is 10 %. Conversely, to prepare 200 g of a 5 % solution you need m₁ = 0.05 · 200 = 10 g of the substance and 190 g of water. If the solution is measured in millilitres, find the mass through the density: m = ρ · V.

Molar concentration

The molar concentration c = n / V shows how many moles of a substance are in a litre of solution. The amount of substance is n = m / M. For 5.844 g of NaCl (M = 58.44 g/mol) in 0.5 L of solution: n = 5.844 / 58.44 = 0.1 mol, c = 0.1 / 0.5 = 0.2 mol/L. You can choose units such as mmol/L or mol/m³, and give the volume in millilitres.

Diluting solutions

On dilution the amount of solute does not change, so c₁ · V₁ = c₂ · V₂. To make 500 mL of a 10 % solution from a 36 % one you need V₁ = 10 · 500 / 36 ≈ 138.9 mL of concentrate and 361.1 mL of water. The law works for any concentration — %, mol/L — as long as c₁ and c₂ use the same units.

Mixing solutions and the cross rule

The amount of solute in a mixture is the sum of the amounts in the two solutions: ω = (ω₁m₁ + ω₂m₂) / (m₁ + m₂). For 100 g of a 10 % and 300 g of a 30 % solution: (10 + 90) / 400 = 25 %. The fraction of the mixture always lies between ω₁ and ω₂. The cross rule answers the reverse question — in what ratio to mix two solutions to get the required fraction: m₁ : m₂ = (ω₂ − ω) : (ω − ω₁) in absolute value.

Converting ω to molarity

To go from a mass fraction to a molar concentration you need the density of the solution: c = 1000 · ω · ρ / M (ρ in g/mL). Concentrated sulfuric acid, 98 % with a density of 1.84 g/mL, has c = 1000 · 0.98 · 1.84 / 98.072 ≈ 18.39 mol/L. Take the density from a reference table — it depends on the concentration and the temperature.

Molality and normality

Molality b = n / m(solvent) is measured in moles per kilogram of solvent: 0.5 mol in 2 kg of water gives 0.25 mol/kg. Unlike molarity it does not depend on temperature. Normality Cₙ = c · z, where z is the number of equivalents: for 1 mol/L of H₂SO₄ in complete neutralisation Cₙ = 2 eq/L.

All numbers are exact: fractions are not rounded (for example 5/36 L), decimals are rounded to the chosen number of places. Atomic masses for formulas come from the settings.

FAQ

How do I find the mass fraction of a solution?

ω = m(solute) / m(solution). For 20 g of salt in 200 g of solution ω = 0.1, or 10 %.

How do I prepare a solution with a given mass fraction?

Find the solute mass as m₁ = ω · m and the water as m₂ = m − m₁. For 200 g of a 5 % solution that is 10 g of the substance and 190 g of water.

How do I find the molar concentration?

c = n / V, where n = m / M. For 5.844 g of NaCl in 0.5 L: c = 0.2 mol/L.

What is the difference between molarity and molality?

Molarity is moles per litre of solution, molality is moles per kilogram of solvent. Molality does not depend on temperature.

How do I calculate a dilution?

By the law c₁V₁ = c₂V₂. The volume of water is V₂ − V₁.

Can I dilute using percentages?

Yes, c₁V₁ = c₂V₂ works for mass fraction too if the volumes refer to solutions of roughly equal density; for exact work switch to masses.

What is the cross rule?

A way to find the mass ratio of two solutions that gives the required fraction: the masses are in the ratio of the differences between the mixture fraction and the fractions of the solutions.

How do I convert percent to moles per litre?

c = 1000 · ω · ρ / M, where ρ is the density of the solution in g/mL.

What is normality?

The number of mole-equivalents in a litre of solution: Cₙ = c · z.

Can I enter the molar mass as a formula?

Yes, in the “M” field you can type a formula (NaCl, H2SO4) and the calculator works out the molar mass itself.

How do I prepare a 0.1 M NaCl solution?

You need 0.1 mol per litre: dissolve 0.1 · 58.44 = 5.844 g of NaCl in water and bring the volume of the solution to 1 L. This very example is in the “Molarity” tab.

Can I mix solutions of different substances?

The “Mixing” tab computes a mixture of two solutions of the same substance. For different substances find the concentration of each separately from the total volume or mass of the mixture.
