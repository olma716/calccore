What BMI is

Body mass index (BMI) is the ratio of weight in kilograms to the square of height in metres: BMI = weight / height². It was devised by the Belgian Adolphe Quetelet and is used today by the WHO and doctors as a quick screen: it shows whether weight fits height but not why. For a person of 75 kg and 178 cm the BMI is 75 / 1.78² ≈ 23.7 — normal.

WHO BMI categories

For adults the WHO distinguishes: below 16 — severe thinness, 16–16.9 — moderate, 17–18.4 — mild; 18.5–24.9 — normal; 25–29.9 — overweight (pre-obesity); 30–34.9 — obesity class I, 35–39.9 — class II, 40 and above — class III. The lower limit belongs to the category: a BMI of exactly 25 is already overweight. The scale on the page shows your position with a marker, and the category table converts the limits into weights for your height.

Normal weight for your height

Normal weight is the weight at which BMI is from 18.5 to 25: weight = BMI × height². For 178 cm that is 58.6 to 79.2 kg and for 170 cm 53.5 to 72.3 kg. The calculator shows how much to gain or lose to reach the lower or upper limit, and the “Normal weight table” tab gives ranges for heights from 150 to 200 cm: for example 150 cm — 42–56 kg, 180 cm — 60–81 kg, 200 cm — 74–100 kg.

Target weight for a desired BMI

The “Target weight” tab computes weight for a chosen BMI: for 178 cm and a BMI of 24 that is 76 kg, i.e. 9 kg below 85 kg, where the BMI is 26.8 (overweight). The middle of the normal range is about a BMI of 22. If the target BMI is outside the normal range, the calculator warns you. For a safe pace of weight change and the calories, use the calorie calculator.

Extra measures: BMI Prime and the new BMI

BMI Prime is the BMI divided by 25 (the upper limit of normal): 0.95 means 95% of the limit, and above 1 is already overweight. The new BMI was proposed by the mathematician Nick Trefethen: 1.3 × weight / height^2.5. It lowers the value for tall people and raises it for short ones: for 190 cm and 80 kg the usual BMI is 22.2 and the new one 20.9; for 150 cm and 55 kg — 24.4 and 25.9. It is a proposal, not a standard, and WHO categories are not defined for it, so here it is for reference only.

Asian action points

In 2004 a WHO consultation kept the international classification but noted that in Asian populations risks may start at lower BMI values, and added public health “action points” — 23.0 and 27.5 (risk is observed from 22 to 25 in different populations, high risk from 26 to 31). These are not official categories, so choosing “WHO + Asian action points” only adds an explanation and the category stays on the standard scale.

Limits of BMI

BMI does not tell muscle from fat: a bodybuilder with a lot of muscle may be “overweight”, and a person with little muscle may be normal with a high body fat percentage. It does not show where fat is stored (waist circumference matters more for risk). For children and teenagers age- and sex-specific percentile charts are used, and for pregnant and older people BMI is less informative. So BMI is a starting point, not a diagnosis: complement it with waist circumference, body fat percentage and a doctor's advice.

BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis: it does not tell muscle from fat and ignores fat distribution. See a doctor to assess your health.

FAQ

How do I calculate BMI?

Divide the weight in kilograms by the square of the height in metres. For 75 kg and 178 cm: 75 / (1.78 × 1.78) ≈ 23.7.

What is a normal BMI?

By the WHO for adults normal is 18.5 to 24.9. Below is thinness, from 25 overweight, from 30 obesity.

How much should I weigh for my height?

Normal weight is a BMI of 18.5 to 25 times the square of the height. For 170 cm that is 53.5–72.3 kg. The calculator shows the exact limits for your height.

Can I trust BMI 100%?

No: it is a screen. BMI does not tell muscle from fat and does not account for where fat is stored, so other measures and a doctor are needed for conclusions.

Why is an athlete's BMI “overweight”?

Muscle is heavier than fat, so a person with a lot of muscle can have a high BMI with a low body fat percentage.

Is BMI suitable for children?

No: for children and teenagers BMI is assessed with age- and sex-specific percentile charts. The adult categories here are only a guide for them.

What are obesity classes I, II and III?

By BMI: 30–34.9 is class I, 35–39.9 class II, 40 and above class III. The risk of chronic diseases grows with each class.

What is BMI Prime?

The BMI divided by 25: it shows what share of the upper limit of normal your BMI is. A value above 1 means overweight.

What is Trefethen's new BMI?

A proposal by the mathematician Nick Trefethen: 1.3 × weight / height^2.5. The value is lower for tall people and higher for short ones; it is a reference measure.

Are the BMI limits the same for all peoples?

The WHO standard is one, but for Asian populations the 2004 WHO consultation added action points of 23 and 27.5, because risks may start at a lower BMI.

How do I set a target weight?

Choose a BMI in the normal range (about 22 is the middle) and multiply it by the square of the height. The “Target weight” tab does it for you.

Why can the number and the category seem different at a boundary?

BMI is rounded to a tenth while the category uses the exact value: 24.95 is shown as 25.0 but still belongs to normal. In such cases the calculator explains it.
