What the equation balancer does

The “Balancing” tab finds the smallest whole-number coefficients for a reaction equation, and “Balance check” counts the atoms of every element on both sides and tells whether the equation is balanced. Write formulas in Latin letters with ordinary digits: Fe2O3, Ca(OH)2, CuSO4·5H2O. Put an arrow between the two sides (->, → or =) and “+” between substances.

Why balance an equation

By the law of conservation of mass the number of atoms of each element is the same before and after the reaction. That is why coefficients are placed before the formulas: they show the molar ratios in which the substances react. Without correct coefficients you cannot do calculations from the equation — finding the masses or volumes of reactants and products.

How the calculator finds the coefficients

The coefficients are named with letters and an equation is written for each element: the number of atoms on the left equals the number on the right. For Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃: Fe: a = 2c, O: 2b = 3c. One variable is taken free, the others are expressed in exact fractions and multiplied by the common denominator — giving a = 4, b = 3, c = 2, that is 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O is found the same way.

Complex and ionic equations

The method works for any equation with a unique solution, including redox ones: 2KMnO₄ + 16HCl → 2KCl + 2MnCl₂ + 5Cl₂ + 8H₂O. In ionic equations the total charge is balanced as well as the atoms: write the charge after ^, for example MnO4^- + Fe^2+ + H^+ -> Mn^2+ + Fe^3+ + H2O, and the calculator finds 1, 5, 8, 1, 5, 4. Write the electron as e^-.

When balancing fails

If some element has no partner on the other side, or the substances are written incorrectly, there is no solution. If there are too many substances and in fact several reactions, there are infinitely many solutions and the calculator asks you to split them into separate reactions. Negative coefficients mean a substance is on the wrong side.

Examples of balanced equations

Combustion of methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. Ammonia synthesis: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Combustion of ethanol: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. Reduction of iron from its ore: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Aluminium with hydrochloric acid: 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂. Each of these can be typed into the calculator (for example Al + HCl -> AlCl3 + H2) to see how the coefficients are found.

How to check the balance by hand

Count the atoms of each element on the left and on the right: for 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O there are 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms on the left and the same on the right. A coefficient multiplies the whole formula while an index multiplies only its own element: 3Ca(OH)₂ contains 3 Ca atoms, 6 O atoms and 6 H atoms. For ionic equations also compare the total charges of the two sides.

The calculator only sets the coefficients: you have to write the products of the reaction yourself, the program does not guess them.

FAQ

How do I balance a chemical equation?

Count the atoms of each element on the left and the right and choose coefficients so the numbers match. The calculator does it automatically.

How do I write formulas in the calculator?

In Latin letters with ordinary digits: H2O, Ca(OH)2, Fe2(SO4)3. Case matters: Co and CO are different substances.

Which arrow should I use?

Either ->, → or = will do. There must be exactly one arrow in the equation.

How do I write the charge of an ion?

After the ^ sign: Fe^3+, SO4^2-, H^+. Write the electron as e^-.

Does the calculator take the charge into account?

Yes: in an equation with ions both the atoms and the total charge are balanced.

What if I have already given coefficients?

In “Balancing” they are ignored and found afresh. To check your own coefficients open “Balance check”.

Why does the calculator say there are several solutions?

Because the equation contains two or more independent reactions. Split it into separate equations.

Does the calculator guess the products?

No, you have to write the products yourself: it only sets the coefficients.

Are states of matter supported?

Yes, you can add (g), (l), (s), (aq): they are shown in the result.

Why are the coefficients whole and the smallest?

That is the standard form: after solving the system the coefficients are multiplied by the common denominator and divided by the greatest common divisor.

Are redox reactions supported?

Yes, if you give all the substances: the method finds coefficients that conserve atoms and charge. Reactions with several independent solutions must be split into separate ones.

How many substances can I enter?

Up to 12 substances in total across both sides of the equation.
