What the earthworks calculator counts

The calculator works out the volume of soil for trenches, pits and areas (up to six sections). It accounts for the slopes by soil type and depth, a working gap, a reserve, and also the loose volume, the weight of the soil, the number of truck trips and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How the volume is counted

A trench with slopes has a trapezoid in cross-section: V = length × depth × (bottom width + m × depth), where m is the slope run per 1 m of depth. A pit with slopes is counted by the prismatoid formula: V = h/6 × (A1 + 4 × Am + A2), where A1 is the bottom area, Am the area of the middle section and A2 the top area. For area × depth, V = area × depth.

The slopes change the result a lot. For a pit of 6 × 8 m and a depth of 2 m with vertical walls it is 96 m³, and with a slope of m = 0.5 it is 126.7 m³, 32% more. The working gap is extra width of the bottom on each side for formwork or waterproofing: it is added to the bottom dimensions.

Slopes by the norm

The calculator picks m by the soil type and the depth of the excavation from the table of SNiP 12-04-2002 (item 5.2.6) for soil above the groundwater level: sand 0.5 / 1 / 1, sandy loam 0.25 / 0.67 / 0.85, loam 0 / 0.5 / 0.75, clay 0 / 0.25 / 0.5, fill 0.67 / 1 / 1.25 (for a depth of up to 1.5 / 3 / 5 m). Deeper than 5 m the slope is set by the design: the calculator takes the value for 5 m and warns about it. This is a Russian norm, so check it against the current Ukrainian one for a project.

Loose volume, weight and trips

After digging the soil loosens and takes up more space: loose volume = volume in the ground × (1 + loosening). The weight does not change with loosening: it is counted from the volume in the ground and the density in the natural state. The number of trips is the larger of two numbers: the loose volume divided by the body and the weight divided by the payload (each rounded up).

Worked example

A 40 m trench with a bottom width of 0.6 m and a depth of 1.8 m and a 3 × 2 m pit 2.5 m deep in loam, a gap of 0.3 m, a slope of m = 0.5: 151.2 m³ and 48.0 m³, 199.2 m³ in total. With a 5% reserve that is 209.1 m³ in the ground. At 22% loosening the loose volume is 255.2 m³, the weight at a density of 1.7 t/m³ is 356 t. For a 10 m³ and 15 t truck that is 26 trips by volume and 24 by weight, so 26. At 150 ₴ per m³ and 1,100 ₴ per trip the cost is 59,971 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real volume depends on the soil, the water level and the way the excavation is made.

FAQ

How do I calculate the volume of a trench?

Multiply the length by the area of the cross-section: depth × (bottom width + m × depth). For a 40 m trench with a bottom width of 0.6 m and a depth of 1.8 m with vertical walls that is 43.2 m³, and with a working gap of 0.3 m on each side and a slope of m = 0.5 it is 151.2 m³.

How do I calculate the volume of a pit with slopes?

By the prismatoid formula: V = h/6 × (bottom area + 4 × the area of the middle section + the top area). The top is larger than the bottom by 2 × m × depth on every side. For a 6 × 8 m pit with a depth of 2 m and a slope of m = 0.5 it is 126.7 m³.

What is a slope and how do I choose it?

A slope describes how far the wall leans off the vertical: m is the run in metres per every metre of depth. It depends on the soil and the depth: for sand up to 1.5 m by the table of SNiP 12-04-2002 m = 0.5, for loam up to 1.5 m m = 0 (vertical walls). If the walls are shored, pick “Vertical walls”.

What is a working gap?

It is extra width of the bottom of a trench or pit on each side so that people can work in it: for formwork, waterproofing or installation. In the calculator it is added to the bottom width, and you set the value.

What is the difference between the volume in the ground and the loose volume?

The volume in the ground is the geometric volume of the excavation. Loose soil takes up more space: for loam with 22% loosening 209.1 m³ in the ground gives 255.2 m³ loose. It is what the trucks haul away.

What loosening percentage should I use?

The sources give about 10–15% for sand, 12–17% for sandy loam, 18–30% for loam and 24–32% for clay. The calculator suggests the middle values, and you can change them.

How much does the soil I haul away weigh?

The weight is the volume in the ground multiplied by the density in the natural state (roughly 1.4–1.9 t/m³ depending on the soil). Loosening does not change the weight. For 209.1 m³ and 1.7 t/m³ that is 356 t.

How many truck trips are needed?

Trips are counted by the larger of two: the loose volume ÷ the body and the weight ÷ the payload. For 255.2 m³ and 356 t, a body of 10 m³ and 15 t that is 26 and 24, so 26 trips.

How do I account for backfill?

In the “Haulage” block enter how much soil (undisturbed volume) stays on site. It is subtracted from the volume to haul away.

Does the calculator account for groundwater?

No. The slopes of the table apply to soil above the groundwater level. If the water is close, the slopes and the shoring are set by the design.

What is the “Area × depth” mode for?

For removing a layer of soil over an area, for example grading or stripping the topsoil: the volume is the area multiplied by the depth, with vertical walls.

How do I calculate the cost of earthworks?

Enter the price per m³ in the ground and the price per trip. The cost = the volume with the reserve × the price + the trips × the price per trip. You enter the prices, none are built in.
