What the drywall calculator counts

The calculator works out how many sheets of drywall are needed for several surfaces (up to six): walls, a ceiling, partitions with sheets on both sides, or a surface with your own area. For every surface you enter the dimensions, the area of the openings and the number of layers, and also the sheet size and the reserve. The result is the number of sheets with a reserve, a picture of their layout and, if you wish, the CD and UD profiles, screws and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How the sheets are counted: layout and area

There are two ways. By area the net area (with the openings subtracted and the layers taken into account) is divided by the area of a sheet and rounded up: this is the lower bound, because the offcuts are assumed to be reused. By layout every surface is counted separately: the sheet is laid along or across and the number of sheets needed to cover the rectangle is taken. The offcuts are not reused in this way and the openings are not subtracted. The real number is between the two.

Example: a wall of 4×2.7 m and a sheet of 1.2×2.5 m. By area that is 10.8 m² : 3 m² = 4 sheets, by layout 6, because the height of 2.7 m is more than 2.5 m and one more row is needed. With a 3.0 m sheet the layout would give 4.

Frame and screws

If you enter the step of the CD profiles, the calculator also counts the frame. For a wall and a partition the CD studs are set along the height with this step, and the UD profile runs along the top and the bottom. For a ceiling the CD profiles run along the length with a step across the width, and the UD goes around the perimeter. The number of profiles is rounded up to pieces of the given length. The screws are counted by the area of the sheets: the area is divided by the product of the profile step and the screw step, multiplied by the number of layers and sides.

For a profile step of 60 cm and a screw step of 25 cm that is 6.7 screws per m² of every layer. The steps depend on your system and the maker, so the fields are empty by default.

Worked example

A wall of 4×2.7 m and a ceiling of 4×3.5 m, a sheet of 1.2×2.5 m, one layer, a 5% reserve. By layout 6 + 6 = 12 sheets, 13 with the reserve; by area it would be 9. 22 CD profiles and 8 UD profiles of 3 m at a 60 cm step, and 166 screws at a 25 cm step. At 600 ₴ per sheet, 110 ₴ per CD, 95 ₴ per UD and 120 ₴ per 100 screws the cost is 11,179 ₴.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. Take the steps of the profiles and screws from the instructions of your system.

FAQ

How do I calculate the number of drywall sheets for a wall?

The area of the wall without openings is divided by the area of a sheet and rounded up. For a more accurate result the calculator also counts the layout: how many sheets are needed to cover the wall with whole sheets.

Why are there more sheets by layout than by area?

Because by area the offcuts are assumed to be reused. For a wall of 2.7 m and a sheet of 2.5 m one more row is needed, and the strip that is left often cannot be used elsewhere. For a wall of 4×2.7 m that is 6 sheets against 4 by area.

Which sheet should I choose: 2.5 or 3 m?

For walls higher than 2.5 m a 3 m sheet is often more convenient: one wall of 4×2.7 m needs 4 sheets against 6 with a 2.5 m sheet by layout. Compare both sizes in the calculator.

How many layers of drywall should I use?

Usually one. Two layers are used where higher strength, sound insulation or fire resistance are needed; it depends on the project. The number of layers is set for every surface.

What does a partition with two sides mean?

Sheets on both sides of one frame. The area of the sheets doubles, and the frame is counted once.

Does the calculator account for doors and windows?

By area the openings are subtracted. By layout they are not: the sheets over the openings are cut and the remainder is rarely usable, so the layout gives a safer upper estimate.

What reserve of drywall should I take?

The reserve is set as a percentage of the number of sheets and rounded up. It covers cutting, breakage and defects. The field is empty by default.

What is the step of the CD profiles?

It depends on the system and the type of the surface, and is given by the maker. So the profile step is entered by you in the calculator, there is no default.

How many screws are needed for drywall?

The area of the sheets is divided by the product of the profile step and the screw step, multiplied by the layers. For steps of 60 and 25 cm that is about 6.7 per m² of every layer.

How many CD and UD profiles are needed?

The CD studs are set at your step along the length of the wall, the UD runs along the top and the bottom. For a ceiling the CD runs along the length with a step across the width, the UD around the perimeter. The number is rounded up to pieces of the given length.

Does the calculator count primer and putty?

No. The calculator counts sheets, profiles and screws. Putty and primer for the walls can be counted separately by the area of the surface.

What sizes of drywall sheets are there?

The most common are 1.2×2.5 m, 1.2×3.0 m and 1.2×2.0 m. Your size is on the sheet; you can enter it as your own.
