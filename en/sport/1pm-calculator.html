What the 1RM is and why you need it

The 1RM (one-rep max) is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good technique in a given exercise: a squat, a bench press, a deadlift. Modern strength programs start from it: working weights are set as percentages of the 1RM and progress is tracked by its change. A direct max test puts a heavy load on joints and ligaments and needs a warm-up and spotters, so the 1RM is usually estimated from a moderate-weight set of several reps.

The formulas used by the calculator

Every formula computes the multiplier “1RM / weight” for r reps. Epley: weight × (1 + r / 30). Brzycki: weight × 36 / (37 − r). Lander: 100 × weight / (101.3 − 2.67123 × r). Lombardi: weight × r^0.10. O'Conner: weight × (1 + 0.025 × r). Mayhew: 100 × weight / (52.2 + 41.9 × e^(−0.055 × r)). Wathen: 100 × weight / (48.8 + 53.8 × e^(−0.075 × r)). You can choose any of them or the mean of all seven. For a single rep the 1RM equals the weight lifted by definition.

Example: 100 kg for 5 reps

For a set of 100 kg × 5 the formulas give: Epley — 116.7 kg, Brzycki — 112.5, O'Conner — 112.5, Lander — 113.7, Wathen — 116.6, Lombardi — 117.5, Mayhew — 119.0; the mean is 115.5 kg. The range from 112.5 to 119.0 kg is about 5.6% of the mean, and that spread shows the real uncertainty of the estimate. That is why the “Compare formulas” tab is useful: it shows the whole range instead of one “exact” number.

The percentage table and the training goal

The “1RM from a set” tab builds a table from 100 to 50% with the working weight, the number of reps the formula matches to that percentage, and the goal. Roughly, 85–100% belongs to strength work (1–5 reps, long rests), 65–84% to hypertrophy (6–12 reps) and below 65% to endurance (12 or more reps). For a 1RM of 116.7 kg by Epley that is 99.2 kg at 85%, 93.3 kg at 80% and 81.7 kg at 70%. Some formulas (Lombardi, Mayhew, Wathen) do not describe very small percentages, and then the table shows a dash.

The four tabs

“1RM from a set” is the main calculation with a table and a chart. “Weight for a set” finds, from your 1RM, the weight for a given percentage or number of reps — for example 80% of 120 kg is 96 kg, or 95 kg rounded to 2.5 kg. “How many reps” estimates from the 1RM and the working weight how many reps you can do: for 90 kg with a 1RM of 120 kg it is about 10. “Compare formulas” shows all seven formulas side by side with the deviation from the mean.

Accuracy and limits

The formulas are empirical: they were derived on samples of healthy people and particular exercises, so accuracy depends on the exercise, technique, fatigue and training status. They work best for 2–10 reps. At 20 reps, for example, 100 kg gives anything from 134.9 kg (Lombardi) to 211.8 kg (Brzycki): the spread is so large that the result is a guide, not a measurement. The calculator accepts up to 30 reps but shows a warning above 12. The result is not medical advice; before a max attempt you need a warm-up, spotters and an experienced coach.

Kilograms, pounds and rounding

The formulas work with any unit of weight, so you can enter pounds and get the answer in pounds; the calculator also shows the equivalent in the other unit (1 lb = 0.45359237 kg). To make the working weights loadable with real plates, choose the rounding: 0.5, 1, 1.25, 2.5 or 5 for kilograms, 1, 2.5, 5 or 10 for pounds. Only the weights in the table and in the set results are rounded; the 1RM itself is shown to a tenth.

FAQ

What is a 1RM?

The one-rep max is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good technique in a given exercise. It is usually estimated from a set of several reps.

How do I calculate a 1RM with the Epley formula?

Multiply the weight by (1 + r / 30), where r is the number of reps. For 100 kg × 5 that is 100 × (1 + 5/30) ≈ 116.7 kg.

Which 1RM formula is the most accurate?

There is no single most accurate one: each is better for certain exercises and rep ranges. That is why the calculator shows the range of all formulas and their mean.

How many reps should I enter for the best accuracy?

From 2 to 10. With one rep the 1RM equals the weight lifted, and above 12 reps the error grows noticeably.

Why do the formulas give different results?

They were derived on different samples and have different shapes. At 5 reps the spread is small (about 6%), at 20 reps it is huge.

How do I find the weight for a set at 80% of the 1RM?

Use the “Weight for a set” tab: enter the 1RM and 80%. For 120 kg that is 96 kg, or 95 kg rounded to 2.5 kg.

How many reps can I do at 75% of the 1RM?

About 10 reps by the Epley and Brzycki formulas. The “How many reps” tab shows the estimate by every formula.

Can I calculate in pounds?

Yes, choose pounds in the “Weight unit” field — the result and the table are in pounds, and the kilogram equivalent is shown separately.

Why round to barbell plates?

So that the working weight matches a real set of plates: for example 116.7 kg cannot be loaded on a bar, but 117.5 kg can.

Which percentages match strength, hypertrophy and endurance?

Roughly: 85–100% strength, 65–84% hypertrophy, below 65% endurance. These are guidelines that differ slightly across the literature.

Is it safe to test a 1RM in practice?

A direct test puts a heavy load on the body and needs a warm-up, spotters and an experienced coach. For planning, a calculation from a set is usually enough.

Why is there a dash instead of reps in the table?

Some formulas (Lombardi, Mayhew, Wathen) do not describe very small percentages of the 1RM within 30 reps, so the reps are not shown for such rows.
