What ROI is and how to calculate it

ROI (Return on Investment) is the profit relative to the money invested: ROI = (value + income − invested) ÷ invested × 100%. If you bought for 100,000 UAH and it is now worth 150,000 UAH, the ROI is 50%.

The calculator also gives the net ROI: after the sale fee, costs and tax. For an example where 100,000 UAH became 150,000 UAH in 3 years with income of 5,000, costs of 1,000, a 1% fee and 23% tax, the ROI before costs is 55% and the net ROI is 40.0% (a net profit of 40,425 UAH).

Why the annual return matters

ROI ignores time: 50% in a year and 50% in 5 years are very different results. So the calculator shows the annual return (CAGR): 50% in 3 years is 14.5% a year, and in the example above the net annual return is 11.9%, and after 8% inflation 3.6%.

That is why compare investments by the annual return: A (100,000 → 130,000 in 2 years) has an ROI of 30% and 14.0% a year, B (100,000 → 150,000 in 5 years) an ROI of 50% but only 8.4% a year, and C (100,000 → 118,000 in a year) 18.0%.

The return needed for a goal

The “Required return” mode solves the reverse problem: which average annual rate you need to reach a goal in a given time. To turn 100,000 UAH with 3,000 UAH contributed a month into 1,000,000 UAH in 10 years you need about 12.3% a year.

To double the money in 10 years you need 7.2% a year (the “rule of 72”: 72 ÷ 10 ≈ 7.2%).

The calculator does not account for risk or the exact payment dates: income is counted at the end of the period. Tax and fee rates are set by you; for selling assets it is usually 18% personal income tax + 5% military levy, but check your own case.

FAQ

How do I calculate the ROI of an investment?

ROI = (value + income − invested) ÷ invested × 100%. For 100,000 UAH that became 150,000 UAH the ROI is 50%. The calculator also shows the net ROI after costs, fee and tax.

How is ROI different from the annual return?

ROI shows the total result without time, while the annual return (CAGR) is the average yearly growth. 50% in 3 years is 14.5% a year, and 50% in 1 year is 50% a year.

How do I account for fees and tax?

Enter the costs, the sale fee and the tax on profit. Example: 100,000 → 150,000 UAH in 3 years with income of 5,000, costs of 1,000, a 1% fee and 23% tax gives a net profit of 40,425 UAH and a net ROI of 40.0%.

What tax applies to investment profit?

For selling assets individuals usually pay 18% personal income tax and a 5% military levy, 23% in total; dividends and some other kinds of income have other rates. The calculator uses the rate you enter and does not charge tax on a loss.

What return do I need to double my money?

About 7.2% a year over 10 years, 14.9% over 5 years, 26.0% over 3 years. The “Required return” mode with a 100% ROI shows it for your term.

How do I compare several investments?

In the “Comparison” mode enter the invested amount, the final value and the term for each. The calculator shows the ROI and the annual return and highlights the best by annual return: in our example it is C (18.0%) against 14.0% and 8.4%.

What is the real return?

It is the return after inflation: (1 + annual return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. With a net annual 11.9% and 8% inflation the real return is 3.6%. A total ROI of 50% in 3 years with 8% inflation is only 19.1% in real money.

What return do I need to accumulate 1,000,000 UAH?

From 100,000 UAH with 3,000 UAH contributed a month over 10 years you need about 12.3% a year. Change the initial amount, the contributions and the term in the “Required return” mode.

Why is ROI not always a good measure?

ROI ignores time, risk and the timing of income. A high ROI over a long time may be weak per year, and a risky investment may lose to a safe one. Compare the annual return and judge the risk separately.

How accurate is the calculator?

It is a simple model: income is counted at the end of the period, so the annual return is approximate; an exact calculation by payment dates gives IRR. The result depends on your assumptions about costs and tax, so it is an estimate, not a guarantee.
