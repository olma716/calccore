Why you need a cooking thermometer

Visual cues (juice color, meat color) are unreliable for determining food safety. The only accurate way to ensure meat is safely cooked is to measure the internal temperature with a cooking thermometer.

How to measure temperature correctly

Insert the thermometer into the thickest part of the food, avoiding bones, fat, and cartilage (they conduct heat differently and give inaccurate readings). For poultry, the best spot is the inner thigh, without touching the bone.

Why rest time matters

After removing meat from heat, it continues cooking due to residual heat (carryover cooking) — the temperature can rise a few more degrees. Resting also allows juices to redistribute evenly throughout the piece, instead of running out when cut immediately after cooking.

Why different foods have different requirements

Poultry (chicken, turkey) requires a higher temperature (74°C/165°F) due to increased salmonella risk. Beef and lamb can be safely eaten at lower temperatures (medium rare, 63°C/145°F), since bacteria are usually only on the surface and are killed during searing. Ground meat always needs full cooking, because bacteria can be mixed throughout the volume during grinding.

Temperature values are based on general food safety guidelines. For immunocompromised individuals, pregnant women, and young children, choosing a higher temperature threshold is recommended.

FAQ

Why is it important to let meat rest after cooking?

During resting, juices redistribute throughout the meat, making it juicier. The temperature also continues to rise slightly (carryover cooking).

Why is the temperature for chicken higher than for beef?

Poultry has a higher risk of salmonella contamination, requiring full cooking to 74°C, while beef can be safely eaten at lower temperatures (medium rare).

Can I rely on the color of meat alone to determine doneness?

No, color is an unreliable indicator — the only accurate way to check meat safety is a cooking thermometer inserted into the thickest part, away from bone.
