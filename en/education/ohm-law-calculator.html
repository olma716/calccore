What the Ohm's law calculator does

The calculator solves the main problems of school electricity. The «Ohm's law» tab finds the rest from any two of the four quantities (voltage U, current I, resistance R, power P). «Resistors» work out the total resistance of series and parallel connections and, given a voltage, the current and power of each resistor. «Energy and cost» turns power and time into energy used and money. «Joule–Lenz» finds the heat Q = I²Rt.

Ohm's law

The current in a section of a circuit is directly proportional to the voltage and inversely proportional to the resistance: I = U / R. Hence U = I · R and R = U / I. For U = 12 V and I = 3 A the resistance is R = 12 / 3 = 4 Ω and the power P = U · I = 36 W. The power can also be found as P = I²R and P = U²/R. The calculator chooses the right formula from what you entered.

Resistors in series

In a series connection the resistances add up: R = R₁ + R₂ + …. The current is the same in all resistors and the voltage divides in proportion to the resistances. For 10, 20 and 30 Ω the total is 60 Ω.

Resistors in parallel

In a parallel connection the conductances add up: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …. The voltage is the same across all resistors and the current divides. For 10, 20 and 30 Ω we get 1/R = 1/10 + 1/20 + 1/30 = 11/60, so R = 60/11 ≈ 5.4545 Ω. The total is always less than the smallest resistor. For two resistors the formula R = R₁R₂/(R₁ + R₂) is handy.

Energy and the cost of electricity

The energy used is A = P · t. An appliance of 2 kW running for 5 hours uses 2 · 5 = 10 kW·h. The cost is the energy in kW·h times the tariff: at 4.32 ₴/kW·h it is 43.2 ₴. Take the tariff from your bill, as it changes. 1 kW·h = 3 600 000 J.

The Joule–Lenz law

The heat released in a conductor carrying a current is Q = I²Rt. For I = 2 A, R = 10 Ω and t = 60 s we get Q = 4 · 10 · 60 = 2400 J. This law explains the heating of wires and the work of electric heaters.

Common mistakes in Ohm's law problems

The most common mistake is mixing up units: resistance in kilohms and current in milliamperes must be converted to ohms and amperes; the calculator does it for you if you choose the unit from the list next to the field. The second is applying Ohm's law to the whole circuit while ignoring the internal resistance of the source: for a section with a resistor I = U / R, but for a full circuit the resistance of the source is added. The third: for non-linear elements such as diodes or incandescent lamps the resistance depends on the voltage, so the simple formula is only an approximation at the operating point.

All numbers are exact: fractions and roots are not rounded (for example 60/11 Ω), and decimals use the chosen number of places.

FAQ

What is Ohm's law?

The current in a section of a circuit equals the voltage divided by the resistance: I = U / R.

How do I find the resistance from the voltage and current?

R = U / I. For 12 V and 3 A the resistance is 4 Ω.

How do I find the power?

P = U · I, and also P = I²R and P = U²/R. For 12 V and 3 A the power is 36 W.

How do I find the resistance of resistors in series?

Add the resistances: R = R₁ + R₂ + … For 10, 20 and 30 Ω it is 60 Ω.

How do I find the resistance of resistors in parallel?

1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … For 10, 20 and 30 Ω it is 60/11 ≈ 5.4545 Ω.

Why is the parallel resistance less than the smallest resistor?

Because in a parallel connection the conductances add up, so the total conductance is larger than any single one.

How do I work out the cost of electricity?

Multiply the power in kW by the time in hours and by the tariff. For 2 kW, 5 h and 4.32 ₴/kW·h it is 43.2 ₴.

How many joules are in a kilowatt-hour?

1 kW·h = 3 600 000 J.

What is the Joule–Lenz law?

Q = I²Rt: the heat in a conductor is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance and the time.

Can I enter values in kilohms, milliamperes and so on?

Yes: every quantity has a unit selector, and the calculator converts to SI exactly.

How do I enter very large or small numbers?

You can use the notation 1.5e3 (that is 1500) or 2e-6 (that is 0.000002).

Why is the answer sometimes written with a root?

Because, for example, U = √(P·R) does not always give a whole number: √50 = 5√2. A decimal value is always shown beside it.

What is 1 ohm?

The resistance of a conductor in which a voltage of 1 V drives a current of 1 A: 1 Ω = 1 V / 1 A.

Can I enter kilohms and milliamperes?

Yes, there is a unit list next to every field (Ω, kΩ, MΩ, A, mA, µA, V, kV…). The conversion is exact and the result can be shown in any unit.
