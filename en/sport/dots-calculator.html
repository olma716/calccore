What is DOTS

DOTS (Dynamic Objective Team Scoring) is a formula that allows comparing the relative strength of powerlifters at different body weights. Since heavier athletes can typically lift a larger absolute weight, DOTS "normalizes" results to fairly compare athletes across different weight classes.

Why DOTS replaced Wilks

The Wilks formula was used for decades, but it was based on outdated strength data. In 2020, the International Powerlifting Federation (IPF) officially switched to DOTS — a newer formula built on more current statistical data, considered fairer for comparison across a wider range of body weights.

How to interpret your score

  • 250+ points: beginner level — typical for those who recently started training.
  • 300+ points: intermediate level — steady training progress.
  • 400+ points: advanced level — serious strength training.
  • 500+ points: elite level — competitive at regional meets.
  • 600+ points: world class — level of top world-ranked powerlifters.

How to use this calculator

Enter your body weight and your total for the three main lifts (squat, bench press, deadlift) — or the result of a single lift if you want to compare just that. The calculator will output your DOTS score, which you can use to compare progress with other athletes regardless of weight class.

DOTS is a comparison tool, not an absolute measure of overall athleticism. It only accounts for strength in the three main powerlifting lifts and doesn't reflect overall fitness or health.

FAQ

What is DOTS?

DOTS is a formula for comparing the relative strength of powerlifters of different body weights. It replaced the Wilks formula as the official IPF standard in 2020.

How is DOTS different from Wilks?

DOTS more accurately accounts for modern strength data across different bodyweight categories and is considered fairer than the older Wilks formula.

What is a good DOTS score?

Roughly: 250+ is beginner, 300+ is intermediate, 400+ is advanced, 500+ is elite, 600+ is world class.
