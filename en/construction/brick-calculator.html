What the brick calculator counts

The calculator works out the number of bricks and the volume of mortar for one or several walls (up to six): every wall has its own length and height (or area), openings area, thickness and number of identical walls. The brick and the joint are shared. The result is bricks with a reserve, bricks per m² of masonry, mortar, pallets and, if you wish, cement and sand for the mortar and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How many bricks per m² of masonry

The number of bricks per m² is (wall thickness + joint) divided by the product (length + joint) × (width + joint) × (height + joint), all in metres. For a single brick 250×120×65 mm and a 10 mm joint that is 51.3 pcs for a half brick (120 mm), 102.6 pcs for one brick (250 mm), 153.8 pcs for one and a half (380 mm) and 205.1 pcs for two (510 mm). These are close to the common guides of about 51, 102, 153 and 205 pcs per m². The joint is yours to set (for example 10 mm); an empty field means a 0 mm joint. In the calculator every wall shows its own bricks per m², so you can check it against these numbers.

Mortar for the masonry

The mortar volume is the volume of the masonry minus the volume of the bricks themselves: the geometric volume of the joints. For a single brick and a 10 mm joint that is 0.020 m³ per m² for a half brick, 0.050 m³ for one brick, 0.080 m³ for one and a half and 0.110 m³ for two, about 0.20 m³ per m³ of masonry. The real consumption is higher (the mortar lies unevenly and soaks into the brick), which the reserve covers.

In the “Mortar mix and materials” block (optional) the dry volume of the mortar is multiplied by a factor (1.3 by default), then divided between cement and sand by the mix, for example 1:4. Cement is counted in kilograms (bulk density 1,440 kg/m³) and rounded up to bags. The defaults are a guide and can be changed.

Worked example

A 10 × 2.7 m wall with 3.6 m² of openings in one brick gives 23.4 m² of net masonry and 2,400 pcs, a 6 × 2.7 m wall with 1.8 m² of openings in a half brick gives 14.4 m² and 738 pcs. In total 3,138 pcs, 3,296 pcs with a 5% reserve and 1.53 m³ of mortar. At 350 pcs per pallet that is 10 pallets. For a 1:4 mortar you need 12 bags of cement of 50 kg and 1.59 m³ of sand.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real consumption depends on the quality of the masonry, trimming and the joint thickness.

FAQ

How many bricks per m² of masonry?

For a single brick 250×120×65 mm and a 10 mm joint: 51.3 pcs for a half brick, 102.6 pcs for one brick, 153.8 pcs for one and a half and 205.1 pcs for two bricks.

How is the number of bricks calculated?

The masonry area is multiplied by (thickness + joint) and divided by the product (length + joint) × (width + joint) × (height + joint). The reserve is added and the result is rounded up.

How many bricks per m³ of masonry?

For a single brick in a one-brick wall (250 mm) it is 410.3 pcs per m³.

How much mortar per m² of masonry?

By the geometry of the joints: 0.020 m³ for a half brick, 0.050 m³ for one brick, 0.080 m³ for one and a half and 0.110 m³ for two bricks. In practice more because of losses, which the reserve covers.

What reserve of bricks and mortar should I allow?

You set the reserve (for example 5%); an empty field means 0%. For the example of two walls that is 3,138 pcs without the reserve and 3,296 pcs with it.

How do I account for windows and doors?

Enter the openings area in m² for each wall: it is subtracted from the masonry area. For a 10 × 2.7 m wall with 3.6 m² of openings the net area is 23.4 m².

How do I count bricks for walls of different thickness?

Add every wall separately with its own thickness (½, 1, 1½, 2 bricks or yours in mm). The calculator sums the bricks and mortar of all the walls.

How do I count bricks of another size?

Pick a ready size (single, thick, double, euro, ceramic block) or “Custom brick” and enter the dimensions in mm. For a euro brick 250×85×65 mm and a wall as long as the brick (250 mm) it is 140.4 pcs per m².

How do I count pallets?

Enter how many bricks are on a pallet. The number of bricks with the reserve is divided by it and rounded up: for 3,296 pcs and 350 pcs per pallet it is 10 pallets (the number per pallet is an example).

How much cement and sand do I need for the mortar?

It depends on the mix and the dry volume factor. For 1.53 m³ of mortar, a 1:4 mix and a factor of 1.3 (a guide) it is 12 bags of cement of 50 kg and 1.59 m³ of sand.

Does the calculator account for trimming, corners and lintels?

No, only the geometry of the masonry. Trimming, corners and breakage are covered by the reserve; enter windows, doors and other openings in the “Openings” field.

Is the calculator suitable for blocks?

Yes, if you enter the block size as “Custom brick”. There is a ready size of 380×250×219 mm for a ceramic block.
