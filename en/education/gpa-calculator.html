What the GPA calculator does

The “GPA from courses” tab computes the average on the US 4.0 scale with pluses and minuses, and the weighted GPA for Honors and AP courses. “Average (Ukraine)” finds the weighted average on the 100-point (ECTS), 5-point or 12-point scale. “Target GPA” shows which average you need for the remaining credits, and the “Grade converter” maps the scales to each other.

How GPA is calculated

GPA is a weighted average: GPA = Σ(grade points · course credits) / Σ credits. A course with more credits affects the result more. For Mathematics (A, 4 credits), Physics (B+, 3), History (A−, 2) and Programming (B, 3) the quality points are 16 + 9.9 + 7.4 + 9 = 42.3 over 12 credits, so GPA = 42.3 / 12 ≈ 3.525, that is ≈ 3.53.

The US 4.0 scale

The most common scale: A = 4.0, A− = 3.7, B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B− = 2.7, C+ = 2.3, C = 2.0, C− = 1.7, D+ = 1.3, D = 1.0, D− = 0.7, F = 0. Some colleges give A+ = 4.3, and others use a scale without pluses and minuses, so check the table of your institution.

Weighted GPA: Honors and AP

In American schools courses of increased difficulty (Honors, AP) may add a bonus to the points: usually +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP when the grade is not F. If Mathematics were Honors, its points become 4.0 + 0.5 = 4.5 and the weighted GPA is ≈ 3.69 instead of 3.53. Bonus rules differ between schools.

Average on the 100-, 12- and 5-point scales

In Ukraine the weighted average by ECTS credits is used for scholarships and rankings. Grades 95, 82, 74 and 90 with weights of 5, 4, 3 and 3 credits give (475 + 328 + 222 + 270) / 15 ≈ 86.33. By the ECTS table this is B (82–89), and in the 5-point scale it is 4. The boundaries of the 100-point scale: A 90–100, B 82–89, C 74–81, D 64–73, E 60–63, FX 35–59, F 0–34; for the 5-point scale: 90–100 = 5, 74–89 = 4, 60–73 = 3.

Target GPA

To bring the GPA to a target, the remaining C₂ credits need an average of Gᵣ = (Gₜ · (C₁ + C₂) − G₁ · C₁) / C₂. For G₁ = 3.2 over 60 credits and a target of 3.4 after another 30 credits you need (3.4 · 90 − 3.2 · 60) / 30 = 3.8. For a target of 3.5 it would be 4.1 — above the 4.0 maximum, so the target is out of reach. The calculator says so and shows the best possible GPA.

All calculations are exact (no floating-point errors). The mappings of the 100-, 12- and 5-point scales to a 4.0 GPA are approximate: there is no single official table.

FAQ

What is GPA?

Grade Point Average — the weighted average of grades. It is the sum of (grade points · credits) divided by the sum of credits.

How do I calculate GPA from courses?

Convert each grade to points, multiply by the course credits, add the results and divide by the total credits.

What are credits?

The weight of a course, usually tied to the number of study hours. The more credits, the more the course affects the GPA.

How does weighted GPA differ from ordinary GPA?

Weighted GPA adds a bonus (for example +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP) to the points of harder courses, so it can exceed 4.0.

How is the average on the 100-point scale calculated?

As a weighted average: Σ(grade · ECTS credits) / Σ credits. The calculator also shows the ECTS letter and the 5-point equivalent of the average.

How do I convert a 100-point grade to a 4.0 GPA?

Through the ECTS letter (A–F) by the chosen mapping table. There is no universal table, so the result is approximate.

What are the ECTS letter boundaries?

A 90–100, B 82–89, C 74–81, D 64–73, E 60–63, FX 35–59, F 0–34.

What GPA do I need for a scholarship or admission?

It depends on the institution: in the US the threshold for graduate school is often 3.0, while Ukrainian universities rank by average grade. Check with your dean's office.

How do I find which average I need next?

Open “Target GPA”: enter the current GPA, the earned and remaining credits and the target.

Are course names kept in the link?

No, the share link keeps only the grades, credits and course type — without names.
