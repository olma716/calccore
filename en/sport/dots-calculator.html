What DOTS is

DOTS (Dynamic Objective Team Scoring) is a formula that turns the total of the three powerlifting lifts (squat, bench press, deadlift) into points taking body weight into account. It lets you compare athletes of different weight classes: a heavier lifter usually lifts more and DOTS “levels” the results. The 2019 formula was created by Tim Konertz of the German federation BVDK as a modern replacement for Wilks; it is used, among others, by USAPL and USPA to decide the best lifter of a meet.

How DOTS is calculated

DOTS = total × 500 / (a·w⁴ + b·w³ + c·w² + d·w + e), where w is body weight in kg and the coefficients are separate for men and women. For example, for a man of 80 kg the coefficient is 0.6895: with a squat of 200, bench of 140 and deadlift of 245 kg (a total of 585 kg) you get 585 × 0.6895 ≈ 403.4. For a woman of 63 kg the coefficient is 1.0755 and a total of 365 kg gives 392.6. The formula is valid for body weights of 40–210 kg (men) and 40–150 kg (women): outside this the result is unreliable, and the calculator warns about it.

DOTS or IPF GL Points

Since 2020 the International Powerlifting Federation (IPF) decides the best lifter by IPF GL Points (Goodlift), not DOTS: DOTS is used by some other federations. That is why alongside DOTS the calculator shows IPF GL for classic (raw) powerlifting by the formula 100 / (A − B·e^(−C·w)) × total. For the same athlete (80 kg, total 585 kg) that is ≈ 82.5 points. The IPF coefficients were valid under a 2020–2023 document, so for official purposes check the current values on the IPF site.

The total needed for a goal

The “Required total” tab inverts the formula: total = DOTS / coefficient. For a man of 80 kg to reach 400 DOTS the total must be about 580.1 kg; for a woman of 60 kg and 300 DOTS about 270.6 kg; for a man of 100 kg and 500 DOTS about 812.3 kg. If you add your current total the calculator shows how far you are, or the margin above the goal.

A table of totals by body weight

The “Table by weight” tab shows the totals that give the chosen DOTS values (300, 400 and 500 by default) for body weights of 50–140 kg for men and 40–110 kg for women. You can change the goals and add your own weight as a separate row. It is handy to see how the required total grows with weight and to plan which weight class to aim for.

DOTS levels: how to read them

There are no official thresholds, and different sources differ by up to about 50 points. The calculator uses a common scale as a guide: for men beginner under 200, intermediate 200–299, advanced 300–399, elite 400–499 and world class from 500; for women under 150, 150–249, 250–349, 350–399 and from 400 respectively. It is an unofficial guide that does not depend on age or federation, so do not treat it as a strict classification.

Limits

DOTS has no separate coefficients for equipped and raw competitions and does not account for age. It compares only powerlifting strength and says nothing about health or general fitness. The calculator shows a “lift DOTS” as a reference figure (lift weight × coefficient), but the official result is calculated from the total of the three lifts.

DOTS and IPF GL are sport formulas for comparing powerlifters of different weights, not a measure of health or general fitness. The levels in the calculator are unofficial guides.

FAQ

What is DOTS?

A formula for comparing the strength of powerlifters of different body weights: the total of three lifts is multiplied by a coefficient based on body weight and sex.

Who created DOTS?

The 2019 formula was developed by Tim Konertz of the German powerlifting federation BVDK. It is used, among others, by USAPL and USPA.

Is DOTS the official IPF formula?

No. Since 2020 the IPF uses IPF GL Points. The calculator shows both so you can compare.

How do I calculate DOTS?

Multiply the total of squat, bench and deadlift by 500 and divide by a polynomial of body weight. For 80 kg and a total of 585 kg it is 403.4.

What DOTS is considered good?

There are no official thresholds. Guides: for men 300+ advanced, 400+ elite, 500+ world class; for women 250+, 350+ and 400+ respectively.

Are the coefficients the same for men and women?

No, the formulas have different coefficients. That is why a woman with the same body weight and total gets more points.

For what body weight is DOTS valid?

For 40–210 kg in men and 40–150 kg in women. Outside this range the result is unreliable, and the calculator shows a warning.

How is DOTS different from Wilks?

DOTS is a newer formula built on more modern data; unlike the old Wilks, it is considered fairer across body weights.

Does DOTS account for age and equipment?

No. DOTS has no coefficients for age or separate ones for equipped and raw competitions.

How do I find the total for a target DOTS?

Divide the target DOTS by the coefficient for your body weight. For 400 DOTS at 80 kg that is about 580.1 kg.

What are IPF GL Points?

The IPF formula for deciding the best lifter: 100 / (A − B·e^(−C·w)) × total. The calculator computes it for classic powerlifting.

Can I calculate DOTS for a single lift?

Officially DOTS is calculated from the total of three lifts. For one lift the calculator gives a reference number, but it should not be compared with official results.
