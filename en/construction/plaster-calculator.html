What the plaster calculator counts

The calculator works out the amount of plaster mix for one or several surfaces (up to six): each has its own length and height (or area), openings area and layer thickness. You can take a ready dry mix (gypsum, cement or with your own consumption from the bag) or count a self-mix from cement and sand. The result is whole bags with a reserve, consumption per m² and, if you wish, water and the cost from your prices. There are no built-in prices.

How a ready mix is counted

The mass of the mix is the net area multiplied by the consumption from the bag (kg/m² per 10 mm) and by the layer thickness divided by 10. For a gypsum mix of 8.5 kg/m² per 10 mm that is 8.5 kg/m² at a 10 mm layer, 12.75 kg/m² at 15 mm and 17 kg/m² at 20 mm. For a cement-sand ready mix of 17 kg/m² per 10 mm it is 17 kg/m² at a 10 mm layer, twice as much. The reserve is added to the mass and the bags are rounded up: a 30 kg bag of gypsum mix covers about 3.5 m² at a 10 mm layer.

The consumption depends a lot on the mix, so the calculator suggests a guide by type, but the most accurate is the figure from the bag of your mix: that is what “Own consumption from the bag” is for.

Self-mix: cement and sand

For a mortar of cement and sand the calculator first counts the volume of the ready mortar (area × thickness), adds the reserve, then multiplies by the dry volume factor (1.3 by default), because cement and sand in the dry state take up more space than the ready mortar. The volume is then split between cement and sand by the mix, for example 1:3. Cement is counted in kilograms (bulk density 1,440 kg/m³) and rounded up to bags.

For a 1:3 mix and a factor of 1.3 that is about 4.7 kg of cement and 9.8 L of sand per 1 m² at a 10 mm layer, or about 468 kg of cement per 1 m³ of mortar, in line with the common guide of 400–500 kg. The defaults can be edited.

Worked example

A 12 × 2.7 m wall with 3.6 m² of openings at a 15 mm layer gives 28.8 m² of net area and 367 kg of gypsum mix, a 20 m² ceiling at a 10 mm layer gives 170 kg. In total 537 kg, 591 kg with a 10% reserve, which is 20 bags of 30 kg. At 380 ₴ per bag and 500 ₴ delivery the cost is 8,100 ₴.

For the same surfaces a 1:3 self-mix with a factor of 1.3: 0.70 m³ of mortar with the reserve, 7 bags of cement of 50 kg (≈ 325 kg) and 0.68 m³ of sand.

The calculator gives an approximate estimate. The real consumption depends on the evenness of the base, the tool and the way the plaster is applied.

FAQ

How much plaster per 1 m² of wall?

It depends on the mix and the layer thickness. For a gypsum mix of 8.5 kg/m² per 10 mm that is 8.5 kg/m² at a 10 mm layer and 12.75 kg/m² at 15 mm. For a cement ready mix of 17 kg/m² per 10 mm it is 17 kg/m² at 10 mm.

How is the amount of plaster calculated?

The net area is multiplied by the consumption from the bag (kg/m² per 10 mm) and by the layer thickness in mm divided by 10. The reserve is added and the bags are rounded up.

How many bags of plaster should I buy?

The amount of mix with the reserve is divided by the bag weight and rounded up. For 591 kg and a 30 kg bag that is 20 bags.

Which mix to choose: gypsum or cement?

The calculator uses a different consumption for them: about 8.5–10 kg/m² per 10 mm for gypsum and 17 kg/m² for cement ready mixes. Take the real consumption from the bag of your mix: that is what “Own consumption from the bag” is for.

How much cement and sand per 1 m² of plaster?

For a 1:3 mix and a dry volume factor of 1.3 it is about 4.7 kg of cement and 9.8 L of sand per 1 m² at a 10 mm layer, and 7.0 kg of cement and 14.6 L of sand at 15 mm.

What cement to sand ratio for plaster?

For plaster 1:3 is the most common (one part of cement to three of sand by volume). The calculator also has 1:4, 1:5 and a “Custom mix” field.

How do I account for windows and doors?

Enter the openings area in m² for each surface: it is subtracted from the area. For a 12 × 2.7 m wall with 3.6 m² of openings the net area is 28.8 m².

How do I count walls and a ceiling with a different layer?

Add every surface separately with its own layer thickness. The calculator sums the mix of all the surfaces and shows a table for each.

What reserve of plaster should I allow?

You set the reserve (for example 10%); an empty field means 0%. An uneven base needs more. For the example of two surfaces that is 537 kg without the reserve and 591 kg with it.

How much water does plaster need?

The amount of water is printed on the bag of a ready mix (L per 1 kg): enter it in the optional field and the calculator counts the total. For a self-mix set the water/cement ratio (W/C).

What is the maximum layer thickness?

It depends on the mix. For Rotband gypsum the manufacturer gives up to 50 mm on a wall in one layer and up to 15 mm on a ceiling. The calculator warns if the layer of a gypsum mix exceeds 50 mm, but follow the data sheet of your mix.

Why is the consumption different on site?

The real consumption depends on the evenness of the base, the average layer thickness, losses when mixing and the application technique. For crooked walls enter a bigger average thickness and a bigger reserve.
