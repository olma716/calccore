Why you need an ingredient substitution finder

It's common to find that you're missing an ingredient, or you want to adapt a recipe for allergies, dietary restrictions, or simply what's in the fridge. This tool helps you quickly find tested alternatives with correct substitution ratios.

Why substitutions are rarely 1:1

Different ingredients vary in moisture, binding properties, sweetness, or fat structure. For example, replacing butter with oil changes the texture of baked goods, and replacing eggs in a vegan recipe affects how the dough holds together. That's why each substitution in our list has its own ratio and a note about the possible effect on the result.

Most popular substitution categories

  • Eggs: for vegan baking or allergies — flax seed, banana, applesauce.
  • Dairy: plant milk instead of cow's milk, yogurt instead of sour cream.
  • Fats: oil instead of butter and vice versa, with differences in taste and texture.
  • Sweeteners: honey, maple syrup, or stevia instead of sugar with ratio adjustments.
  • Leavening agents: how to make baking powder from baking soda and cream of tartar if you're out.

It's recommended to test a substitution on a small batch before applying it to the whole recipe — the result may vary slightly depending on the specific dish.

FAQ

Do substitutions always work 1:1?

No, most substitutions have their own ratio, listed for each option. Some substitutions also affect the taste or texture of the dish.

Can I combine multiple substitutions in one recipe?

Yes, but each substitution changes the result slightly. If you substitute several ingredients at once, the result may differ more than with a single substitution.

Are these substitutions suitable for vegan recipes?

Some substitutions (like flax seed or banana for eggs) are popular specifically for vegan baking and are marked accordingly.
