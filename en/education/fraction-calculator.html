What the fraction calculator does

The calculator works in six modes: operations with fractions (add, subtract, multiply, divide 2–10 fractions and mixed numbers), reducing, converting a mixed number to an improper fraction and back, comparing up to five fractions, converting a decimal to a fraction and back (including repeating decimals), and bringing fractions to a common denominator. Every result comes with a step-by-step solution and is shown at once in three forms: a fraction, a mixed number and a decimal.

How to do operations with fractions

To add or subtract fractions, bring them to a common denominator (the least common multiple of the denominators) and then add the numerators. For example, for 3/4 + 5/6: LCM(4, 6) = 12, so 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12 = 1 7/12 = 1.58(3). To multiply, multiply the numerators and the denominators; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal. The result is reduced by the GCD of the numerator and the denominator.

The order of operations is the same as in ordinary maths: multiplication and division come before addition and subtraction. The expression 1/2 + 2/3 × 3/4 equals 1, because 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2 comes first. If you go left to right instead, you get 7/8. The calculator lets you choose either.

Mixed numbers, reducing, decimals

A mixed number w n/d becomes an improper fraction by (w·d + n)/d, for example 2 3/5 = 13/5. In reverse, 17/5 = 3 2/5, because 17 ÷ 5 = 3 with a remainder of 2. To reduce, divide the numerator and the denominator by their GCD, which the calculator finds with Euclid's algorithm: 48/18 has a GCD of 6 and reduces to 8/3.

The decimal 0.75 equals 75/100 = 3/4. The repeating 0.58(3) equals (583 − 58)/900 = 7/12: the number with the period minus the number without it, over nines (for the period) and zeros (between the point and the period). A fraction becomes a decimal by long division, and when a remainder repeats a period appears: 1/3 = 0.(3), 7/12 = 0.58(3).

Comparing and the common denominator

To compare fractions, bring them to a common denominator and compare the numerators. For 3/4, 5/6 and 9/12 the LCM of the denominators is 12: 9/12, 10/12 and 9/12, so 3/4 = 9/12 < 5/6. The same step is needed to add fractions: for 5/6, 3/8 and 7/12 the common denominator is 24 and the new numerators are 20, 9 and 14.

All calculations are exact: fractions are stored as whole numbers with no decimal rounding.

FAQ

How do I add fractions with different denominators?

Find the least common denominator (the LCM of the denominators), bring the fractions to it and add the numerators. For 3/4 + 5/6 the common denominator is 12, so 9/12 + 10/12 = 19/12.

How do I subtract fractions?

The same way as adding: bring them to a common denominator and subtract the numerators. If a larger fraction is taken from a smaller one, the result is negative.

How do I multiply fractions?

Multiply the numerators, multiply the denominators, then reduce. For 2/3 × 3/4 that is 6/12 = 1/2.

How do I divide fractions?

Replace division with multiplication by the reciprocal: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c. You cannot divide by zero, and the calculator warns you.

What is the order of operations with fractions?

Multiplication and division first, then addition and subtraction, left to right. The expression 1/2 + 2/3 × 3/4 equals 1, not 7/8. The calculator can also go one by one like an ordinary calculator.

How do I convert a mixed number to an improper fraction?

Multiply the whole part by the denominator, add the numerator and keep the denominator: 2 3/5 = (2·5 + 3)/5 = 13/5.

How do I convert an improper fraction to a mixed number?

Divide the numerator by the denominator with a remainder. The quotient is the whole part and the remainder becomes the numerator: 17/5 = 3 2/5.

How do I reduce a fraction?

Find the GCD of the numerator and the denominator and divide both by it. For 48/18 the GCD is 6 and the result is 8/3. If the GCD is 1, the fraction cannot be reduced.

How do I compare fractions?

Bring them to a common denominator and compare the numerators. The “Compare” mode does it for 2–5 fractions and orders them ascending or descending.

How do I convert a decimal to a fraction?

Write the digits after the point as the numerator and put 1 and as many zeros as there are digits after the point in the denominator, then reduce: 0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4. For repeating decimals write the period in brackets, for example 0.(3) = 1/3.

What is a repeating decimal?

A decimal in which a group of digits repeats forever: 1/3 = 0.(3), 1/7 = 0.(142857). The calculator finds the period by long division and shows it in brackets.

Are the calculations exact?

Yes. Fractions are stored as whole numbers (up to 30 digits in every field) with no decimal rounding, so large numerators and denominators keep their precision.
