What are GCD and LCM

The GCD (greatest common divisor) is the largest natural number that divides all the given numbers without a remainder. The LCM (least common multiple) is the smallest natural number that is divisible by each of them. For 12 and 18: GCD = 6, LCM = 36.

How to find the GCD: the Euclidean algorithm

Divide the larger number by the smaller one, then the smaller one by the remainder, and repeat until the remainder is zero. The last non-zero remainder is the GCD. For 48 and 18:

  • 48 = 2 · 18 + 12
  • 18 = 1 · 12 + 6
  • 12 = 2 · 6 + 0 — so GCD(48, 18) = 6

How to find the LCM

For two numbers LCM(a, b) = a · b / GCD(a, b). Another way is prime factorization: take every prime with its largest exponent. For 12 = 2² · 3 and 18 = 2 · 3² we get LCM = 2² · 3² = 36. The calculator shows both methods for every set of numbers.

Where GCD and LCM are used

  • Simplifying fractions: divide the numerator and denominator by their GCD.
  • Common denominator: when adding fractions, use the LCM of the denominators.
  • Repeating events: the moment two periodic processes coincide again is the LCM of their periods.
  • Splitting into equal groups: the largest group size that fits every quantity is the GCD.

FAQ

What is the GCD?

The GCD (greatest common divisor) is the largest natural number that divides several numbers without a remainder. For example, GCD(12, 18) = 6.

What is the LCM?

The LCM (least common multiple) is the smallest natural number that is divisible by each of the given numbers without a remainder. For example, LCM(12, 18) = 36.

How are the GCD and LCM related?

For two numbers, the product of the GCD and LCM equals the product of the numbers: GCD(a, b) · LCM(a, b) = a · b. So LCM = a · b / GCD.

How do I find the GCD of three or more numbers?

Find it step by step: GCD(a, b, c) = GCD(GCD(a, b), c). The calculator does this automatically for several numbers (up to 20).

Which numbers are called coprime?

Numbers whose GCD equals 1, meaning they share no divisors other than one. For example, 8 and 15.
