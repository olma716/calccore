                How the EV charging calculator works

                The calculator estimates the cost of charging an electric car and the approximate time in three modes: home socket, charging station and comparison of two options. Enter the battery capacity (or pick a car model — the calculator fills in an approximate capacity), current and target charge, losses and tariff — it does the rest.

                The formulas: energy into the battery = capacity × (target % − current %), energy from the grid = energy into the battery ÷ (1 − losses), cost = energy from the grid × tariff (for per-minute stations — time × tariff).

                Realistic charging time

                Charging time depends not only on the socket or station power but also on how much power the car itself accepts. In “Car parameters” you can enter the maximum AC (onboard charger, often 7–11 kW) and maximum DC power — the calculator uses the lower of the two values. At fast DC stations the power drops sharply after 80% charge, so for that part the calculator roughly assumes a slowdown to ~30% of the power.

                Cost per 100 km, range and monthly costs

                Enter the car consumption (kWh/100 km from the trip computer) — and the calculator shows the cost per 100 km and the range this charge gives. Add the mileage per month to get charging costs per month and per year. To compare with petrol use the fuel cost calculator and the TCO calculator. For the range in different conditions see the EV range calculator.

                Session fee and finish time

                Enter the start time (for example, 23:00) and the calculator shows when charging will finish. For stations you can add a session fee (a one-off connection charge) — it is included in the cost and in the cost per 100 km.

                Electricity tariffs in 2026

                Household electricity is currently often quoted at a fixed 4.32 UAH/kWh; reportedly the government extended it until 31 October 2026, so the price may change after that date. For a two-zone meter at night (23:00–07:00) 2.16 UAH/kWh applies. For electric heating in the heating season — 2.64 UAH/kWh for the first 2,000 kWh per month.

                Public charging stations use commercial operator tariffs that depend on the network, charging type (AC/DC), location and time, so it is best to enter the tariff from your operator’s app. Fast DC charging is considerably more expensive than charging at home.

                Tip: for the most accurate result, enter the tariff from your supplier’s bill or the charging network app.

                FAQ

                How much does it cost to charge an electric car at home?

                For a 62 kWh battery from 20% to 80% with 10% losses about 41.3 kWh is drawn from the grid. At 4.32 UAH/kWh that is roughly UAH 179, and at a night tariff of 2.16 UAH about UAH 89. At a fast station at 24 UAH/kWh the same charge would cost about UAH 992. Enter your own data in the calculator for an exact result.

                How long does an electric car take to charge from a socket?

                For the same 20% to 80% charge (about 41.3 kWh from the grid) a regular 2.3 kW socket takes about 18 hours, a 7.4 kW socket about 5.6 hours and 11 kW about 3.8 hours. The real time depends on the car’s maximum AC power, which you can enter in the calculator.

                Why is “from the grid (with losses)” higher than “into the battery”?

                Part of the energy is lost to current conversion, heat, electronics and battery balancing. So more kWh is drawn from the grid than actually ends up in the battery.

                What does “estimated charging time” mean and why can it differ in reality?

                Time is energy from the grid ÷ power, taking the car’s limits (if entered) and an approximate DC slowdown after 80% into account. In reality speed is affected by temperature, battery condition, station and cable limits.

                How does a “per minute” station tariff work?

                In “per minute” mode the calculator estimates the charging time and multiplies it by the tariff. This tariff is used at some stations, so slower charging costs more.

                Does the choice of socket/power affect the cost?

                On a home tariff the price per kWh does not change with power — mostly the time changes. But on a per-minute tariff slower charging costs more.

                What charging losses should I use if I don’t know?

                A starting value of 10% works. For slow charging from a socket it is often 5–15%, and for other cars and conditions it can differ.

                Where do the battery capacities for the car models come from?

                These are usable (net) capacities from public sources: EV specification databases and manufacturer data. For some models (Tesla, Chinese cars, different years and trims) the sources disagree, so the value is approximate — check it for your car against its documents and correct it manually if needed. Charging power and car consumption are not filled in because they depend on year, market and trim.

                Where can I find the car consumption (kWh/100 km)?

                On the trip computer or in the car app — the average consumption over recent trips. In the city it is usually lower than on a highway at high speed, and higher in winter because of heating and battery temperature.

                How do I calculate the charging cost per 100 km?

                Enter the car consumption in kWh/100 km. The calculator multiplies it by the real price of a kWh into the battery (including losses and tariff) — that is the cost per 100 km.

                How do I calculate monthly charging costs?

                Enter the car consumption and the monthly mileage in km. The calculator shows costs per month and per year for the chosen charging option, or for both in compare mode.

                What are the car’s maximum AC and DC power?

                AC is charging with alternating current from a socket or an AC station, limited by the onboard charger (often 7–11 kW). DC is fast charging at stations with direct current, limited by the battery and the car’s electronics. Enter the values from the car’s specifications to get a more accurate time.

                What is a session fee?

                Some stations charge a one-off connection fee regardless of the kWh delivered. Enter it and it is added to the charging cost, and therefore to the cost per 100 km and monthly costs.

                Is my data stored?

                Only if you turn on “Remember my data”. The values are then stored only in your browser on this device and are never sent anywhere. Turn the option off or press “Reset” to delete them.

                Why is DC charging slower after 80%?

                To protect the battery the car sharply reduces power as the charge approaches full, so the last percent at a fast station takes longer. The calculator accounts for this approximately: for the part above 80% about 30% of the power is used.
