    What is TCO and why does it matter?

    TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) is the full cost of owning a car over a chosen period: purchase price + energy (fuel or electricity) + service, insurance, tax and tyres, minus the money you get from reselling the car. This gives a far more realistic picture than comparing fuel or electricity consumption alone.

    For example, an EV can be much cheaper to run every day but have a higher purchase price. TCO shows under which conditions (mileage, energy prices, service, residual value) an EV becomes cheaper than a gasoline car — or the other way round.

    What the calculator includes

    For the ICE car you can choose the fuel — petrol, diesel or LPG: the labels and hints in the ICE card adapt to the chosen fuel, and you enter the fuel price yourself. Car price, fuel or electricity consumption and price, service, insurance, tax and tyres per year, and the residual value (resale) at the end of the period, which is subtracted from the total. You can also turn on a loan (down payment, rate, term): interest paid during ownership is added to the TCO, and for the EV you can set the share of home and public charging with different tariffs. Not included: repairs outside scheduled service and the cost of a home charger.

    How to read the result

    The calculator shows the total cost of both cars, the difference between them, the cost per 1 km, the payback of the price gap (in kilometres and years), the cost breakdown, a cumulative cost chart with the crossing point and a year-by-year table. It is handy for comparing different cars at the same yearly mileage.

    Currencies and units

    You can choose a currency — UAH, USD, EUR, GBP, CHF, PLN, CZK, HUF, RON, CAD, AUD or TRY: amounts you have entered are converted at the NBU rate. Mileage can be entered in kilometres or miles, fuel consumption in L/100 km, km/L, L/100 mi or mpg (US and UK), fuel price per litre or per gallon, EV consumption in kWh/100 km, kWh/100 mi, mi/kWh or km/kWh.

    FAQ

    Why is an EV sometimes more expensive even if electricity is cheaper?

    Most often because of the higher purchase price. With low mileage the energy savings may not be enough to cover the price difference.

    Which costs are included and which are not?

    Included: purchase price, energy (fuel or electricity), service, insurance, tax and tyres per year, plus the residual value (resale) at the end of the period. Loan interest (during ownership) is added if you fill in the “Loan” block. Not included: repairs outside scheduled service and a home charger.

    What is residual value and how do I enter it?

    It is the share of the price you get back by selling the car at the end of the ownership period. Enter it as a percentage of the purchase price (for example, 40). This amount is subtracted from the total cost; if the field is empty, resale is not counted.

    Can I calculate for diesel or LPG?

    Yes. In the “General data” block choose the ICE fuel: petrol, diesel or LPG. The labels and hints in the ICE card adapt to the chosen fuel. Enter the fuel price yourself because it changes often — this keeps the result accurate.

    How do I choose a currency?

    Pick a currency in the “General data” block: UAH, USD, EUR, GBP, CHF, PLN, CZK, HUF, RON, CAD, AUD or TRY. Amounts you have entered are converted at the NBU rate, and all results are shown in the selected currency.

    Can I enter miles, mpg and gallons?

    Yes. Each field has a unit selector: mileage in km or miles, fuel consumption in L/100 km, km/L, L/100 mi or mpg, fuel price per litre or gallon, EV consumption in kWh/100 km, kWh/100 mi, mi/kWh or km/kWh. The number you typed does not change when you switch the unit.

    Can I enter fractional values with a comma?

    Yes. You can type 1,6 or 1.6 — the calculator handles both formats.

    What is “cost per 1 km” in the results?

    It is the total cost for the period divided by the total mileage over that period. It makes it easier to compare different scenarios and cars.

    How is payback calculated?

    The purchase price gap (the EV costs more) is divided by the yearly saving on energy, service, insurance, tax and tyres. The result is shown in kilometres and years at current prices; resale and loan are not counted in payback.

    How do I include a loan?

    Open the “Loan” block and enter the down payment in percent, the rate and the term. The calculator adds the interest you pay during ownership to the TCO (same terms for both cars); the remaining debt is paid off from the resale.

    How do I include home and public charging?

    Enter the home electricity price, the public charging price and the share of charging at home in percent. The calculator uses the weighted average price. If you leave the share empty, it assumes you charge only at home.
