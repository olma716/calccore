What are macros

Macros (macronutrients) — protein, fat, and carbohydrates — are the three main components that make up the calorie content of any food. Each plays different roles in the body and has a different energy value: protein and carbs provide 4 kcal per gram, fat provides 9 kcal per gram.

Protein: the building material

Protein is made of amino acids used for repairing and building muscle tissue, synthesizing enzymes and hormones, and supporting the immune system. Active individuals and those doing resistance training should aim for 1.6–2.2g of protein per kg of body weight per day. Sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, tofu.

Carbs: the main energy source

Carbohydrates are the primary fuel for the brain and muscles during intense training. They're divided into simple (quickly absorbed, raise blood sugar fast) and complex (fiber, slow absorption, longer satiety). For athletic performance, complex carbs are generally preferred: grains, whole-grain products, vegetables, fruit.

Fat: hormones and vitamin absorption

Fats are involved in hormone production (including testosterone), absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), and maintaining cell membranes. There are saturated fats (limit), unsaturated fats (beneficial — olive oil, avocado, nuts), and trans fats (avoid). Even during aggressive weight loss, it's not recommended to drop fat intake below ~20% of total calories.

How macros are distributed by goal

  • Weight loss: higher protein (to preserve muscle during a calorie deficit) and moderate carbs.
  • Maintenance: a balanced split — roughly 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat.
  • Muscle gain: enough protein for muscle growth plus more carbs for training energy.

Practical tips for hitting your macro targets

  • Weigh your food for the first few weeks — this helps you learn to estimate portions by eye.
  • Plan meals in advance — it's easier to stay on target when you have a plan for the day.
  • Spread protein evenly throughout the day (every 3-4 hours) for better absorption.
  • Don't stress over small fluctuations — ±5-10g per day isn't critical; weekly consistency matters more.

How much protein do I need per day?
